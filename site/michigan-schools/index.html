Spending and graduation rates.

Each dot is a district: General Fund spending per pupil (2024–25) against its four-year graduation rate (class of 2025). Color shows the share of students who are economically disadvantaged. Click a dot to open that district.

Read with care. This chart shows how two numbers line up; it does not show that spending causes graduation rates to rise or fall. Family income is strongly linked to test scores and graduation rates, which is why poverty level is shown, and districts with more students in poverty often receive more state and federal money per pupil for extra services. Districts without a graduation cohort (for example, no high school) or with fewer than 20 students in the cohort are left off; many of the lowest rates are at online, credit-recovery and alternative-education charter schools, and choosing “Traditional” removes most of them. Dashed lines are state medians.
Sources: MDE Bulletin 1014 (2024–25); CEPI 2025 Graduation and Dropout Report; CEPI Fall 2024 Economically Disadvantaged counts.

Notes & sources.

All figures are as reported by the state for the 2024–25 school year. Nothing here is adjusted, estimated or ranked by this site beyond simple medians and percentiles.

  • Revenue and spending per pupil are General Fund figures from MDE Bulletin 1014 (2024–25 data file), divided by the district's fall pupil count (FTE). They exclude debt, building and other separate funds, so bond-funded construction does not appear here.
  • Local / state / federal shares are Bulletin 1014's revenue categories. "Local" includes property taxes and money received from the intermediate school district (ISD) and other Michigan public sources. Charter schools cannot levy property taxes, so their local share is usually small.
  • Spending by area uses Bulletin 1014's function totals. Support services include administration, operations and maintenance, transportation, pupil and instructional-staff support and other services. "Other" is total General Fund spending minus instruction and support services (for example community services, capital outlay and transfers).
  • Fund balance is the ending General Fund balance from MDE Bulletin 1011 (2024–25), shown as a share of that year's total General Fund expenditures and transfers out.
  • Building-level spending comes from CEPI's School-Level Expenditure Report (2024–25 download file). It covers all funds except state-defined exclusions, so it is not directly comparable to the General Fund figures. "Districtwide" spending is not tied to a building and is split equally per pupil across all of a district's buildings.
  • District type. Charter schools (public school academies, PSAs) are identified by their state district code (codes ending 700–799 or 900–999). ISD names come from CEPI's 2024–25 Financial Information Database revenue file.
  • Medians and percentiles count each district once, regardless of size. "Higher than X%" means the share of the 821 districts with a lower value; "lower than X%" means the share with a higher value. Very small districts and online charter schools can have unusual per-pupil figures.
  • Graduation rate is the four-year cohort rate for the class of 2025 (students who first entered 9th grade in fall 2021, adjusted for transfers) from CEPI's 2025 Graduation and Dropout Report (data file). Districts without a high school have no rate. The statewide rate is 84.0%.
  • Economically disadvantaged is the share of a district's K-12 students identified as eligible for free or reduced-price meals or otherwise economically disadvantaged, fall 2024, from CEPI's Economically Disadvantaged counts (data file). Enrollment change uses the total K-12 count in the fall 2021 and fall 2025 versions of the same file.
  • Correlation is not causation. Spending, poverty, district size and outcomes are all related. Higher-poverty districts often receive extra per-pupil funding for added needs, and family income is strongly linked to test scores and graduation. Compare districts with similar poverty levels before drawing conclusions.
  • Test scores are not included yet. MI School Data delivers its district-level M-STEP, PSAT and SAT proficiency files by email request rather than as a public direct download, so they are not part of this version.
  • Missing: the 2024–25 bundle on MI School Data's Financial Data Files page contains revenue, balance-sheet and ESP files but not the detailed expenditure file, so spending by object (salaries, benefits, purchased services) is not shown yet. Building-level figures cover all buildings the state reports; some districts report few or none.

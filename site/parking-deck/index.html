Executive summary

The decision in one screen

The proposal links a parking structure at EGR High School with traffic signals and pedestrian infrastructure. The case turns less on today’s utilization than on what happens when school construction removes spaces from 2028–2030.

$15.2M–$18.1M

What is proposed

A three-level, 240-space deck, plus signals and pedestrian work. The City would bond up to $9M; EGRPS has discussed roughly $7M toward the project.1

371 empty

What the counts found

The 2025 study’s system-wide peak was 71.7%: 939 of 1,310 spaces occupied. School lots reached 81% on a weekday and 86.8% on Saturday.8

~190 net

What the deck adds

After accounting for the surface lot it replaces, the deck adds about 190 spaces versus the post-construction, no-deck condition. inferred1

Bottom line

The strongest need case is a projected 2028–2030 construction-period shortage, not present-day system overcrowding. The strongest unresolved issues are the unsigned city–school agreement, long-term upkeep, and the lack of binding commitments on street-parking removal or free use.

01NeedCurrent capacity is available; construction changes the campus math.

The 2025 utilization counts do not show a system-wide shortage on the measured dates. At the busiest observed system hour, 939 of 1,310 spaces were occupied—71.7%—leaving 371 open.8 The school lots reached 81% on the measured weekday and 86.8% on Saturday; the commonly used 85% benchmark was not reached system-wide on the weekday.816

Where the shortage comes from

The high-school expansion changes the campus supply. The senior lot had 111 spaces; the expansion plan retains 40, creating a 71-space reduction.5 That issue originates in the separately approved $158.9M school bond program, rather than in a measured shortage across the full district today.5

Key primary-source finding
“Constructing the High School expansion without any parking improvements.”

That was the Progressive Companies memo’s recommendation. It found on-street capacity within 1,500 feet and said a structure should be considered only after other options were explored. It also warned that a 30–60 minute exit could push students back to on-street parking.5

An industry benchmark cited in the record is roughly $42,000 all-in per structured space.17 Applied to 240 spaces, that implies a baseline near $10.1M; the $15.2M–$18.1M project range carries an estimated premium of roughly $5M–$8M. inferred The record points to architectural treatment, heated walks and an elevator among the added features, but provides no released line-item budget to isolate each premium.1

Annual ownership cost

The operating benchmark supplied by the City is $611 per space per year, or about $147,000 for 240 spaces. inferred The source explicitly excludes depreciation.6 Adding a planning allowance equal to about 1% of construction cost for major repairs and renewal produces a full-cost range of roughly $300,000–$330,000 per year. inferred A 50% City share would be about $150,000–$165,000 annually. No dedicated funding source is identified in the reviewed materials.

What $160K means

The City’s stated estimate is consistent with routine operations for the whole structure. It is not a full lifecycle estimate if depreciation and capital repair reserves are excluded.6

The City reported about $6.5M in unassigned general-fund balance, equal to 44% and above its 20%–25% target.1 Using reserves could reduce borrowing, but paying the full City share from that balance would substantially reduce the cushion and would not solve ongoing operating costs.

Local tax context

EGR’s 2024 operating levy was 14.09 mills—high among nearby Kent County cities, though below Cedar Springs at 18.77 and Lowell at 15.95.1 This comparison is context, not a measure of service equivalence or total household tax burden.

04Governance & processThe proposal moved from design funding to ballot language in under four months.
APRIL 20, 2026

Design funding approved, 5–2

The early recommendation described a smaller 213-space concept; underground parking was rejected for “safety concerns.”2

JULY 20, 2026

Financing routes reviewed

The Commission considered limited-tax general-obligation bonds, which would not require a vote, and chose to proceed with voter-approved bonds.4

AUGUST 3, 2026

Postponement failed; ballot placement passed

The postponement motion failed 2–4; Commissioner Hunter left at 8:23 p.m. The ballot-placement vote passed 4–1–1, with Wessely voting no and Groff-Blaszak abstaining.1

AUGUST 6, 2026

Ballot typo corrected, 4–1–2

A special meeting corrected the millage language. This 4–1–2 record is separate from the August 3 ballot-placement vote.1

Subcommittee record

Meetings stayed below quorum, so no minutes exist. The public record does not show how key project choices were made.

Late resolution

A revised 33-page resolution arrived about 90 minutes before the August 3 vote and was not public beforehand.1

Pedestrian scope

Pedestrian-safety language was added in August, but no project list or dedicated budget was released.1

Agreement not signed

No executed intergovernmental agreement was identified. Cost share, ownership, access and the City’s third-level role therefore remain nonbinding.

Gaslight history

The debate is shaped by the loss of a prior private parking structure in Gaslight Village and earlier development commitments. That history explains public skepticism, but it does not by itself establish the need, cost or legal obligations of this proposal. The current decision should be judged on the current agreements and data.

07Risks & open questionsThe ballot sets borrowing authority; several implementation choices remain open.

Cost escalation

The estimate assumes roughly 3.5% annual escalation—more than $500,000 for a year of delay on a $15M-plus project. inferred1

Interest-rate exposure

The modeled true interest cost rose from 3.33% to 4.13% across estimates. Final debt service depends on market conditions when bonds are sold.7

Undefined extras

Signal work, loading-dock accommodation, snow-melt systems and final facade choices are not fully itemized in the released estimate.

Behavior risk

No student survey tests whether drivers will choose the deck, especially if departure queues are long or event pricing is later introduced.

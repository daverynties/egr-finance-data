# Round 2 — Grok

**Where I agree**
- All three of us keep the certified question on the ballot and put hard gates in front of any bond sale. The gates are a signed city–school agreement, an itemized estimate backed by bids, a funded operating plan and reserve, and a costed safety project list.
- The need is real but narrow: 71 spaces lost and about 65 on campus in 2028–30. Area-wide, 371 spaces sat empty at the peak (traffic memo; utilization CSV; Sept 30 letter via dossier §5).
- Claude: cap the city's share at the plain-concrete Option 5 unless the schools pay for the brick. I'm adding this.
- Codex: the 2028–30 student access plan has to go ahead whatever the vote, and the Feb 4 "may not be feasible" caveat must be explained in writing. I'm adding both.

**Where I disagree and why**
- **Codex's "pause."** The commission can't pause the vote. If it passes, the gates already hold back borrowing. The real difference is whether a priced comparison of alternatives is a precondition. I now agree it should be one gate, but "pause" tells voters the city is withdrawing, and it isn't.
- **Meeting dates.** Codex puts the commission's action on Oct 26 and Claude says "next regular meeting." The inputs don't give the fall meeting calendar, so the date is for the City Clerk to confirm. The resolution's binding effect on future commissions is for the city attorney to confirm.
- **Claude's ~$300–330K a year with reserve.** That figure rests on Walker benchmarks from secondary sources. The primary files support about $56K–$147K a year before any capital reserve (operating-cost emails; dossier update). We should show both and label the source of each.
- **My own Round 1** didn't say what happens if the schools can't fund their half. I'm adding: if bids put the schools' half above their ~$7M set-aside (Apr 20 board minutes via dossier §1) and they don't commit the difference, no bonds are sold.

**Revised position**
Proceed with conditions, plus a downsizing test: no bond sale until all gates are met. The gates now include a priced comparison of alternatives, with a two-level or Option 5 version among them. Borrowing is sized to the bids, and the interim 2028–30 access plan goes ahead whatever the vote.

# Joint recommendation — East Grand Rapids parking-deck bond (Nov 3, 2026)
**Signed:** Grok · Claude (`claude-opus-5-5`) · Codex (`gpt-6.1-sol`, high effort). All three signed after Round 3 edits; no dissent recorded.

*Grok, Claude and Codex acting as hypothetical city leaders. This is an AI exercise, not an official city position. Facts come from the input record; "(inferred)" marks our own arithmetic or judgment; "(to confirm with city attorney)" marks legal mechanics the record doesn't establish.*

**Decision.** Let the certified Nov 3 question go to voters. Make no bond sale or construction award until all published gates are met. Any earlier cost-share agreement must condition financial obligations on voter approval and satisfaction of the remaining gates. One gate is a priced comparison with smaller and cheaper options. Choose the option the comparison supports, including an alternative without a deck. Before proceeding with bond financing, the City Attorney must confirm that the selected project and borrowing fit the certified voter authorization; borrowing is sized to the bids. Separately, deliver a 2028–30 student-access plan whatever the vote.

**Why**
- **Real but narrow need.** The expansion removes 71 spaces (traffic memo, Nov 10, 2025), and the campus drops to about 65 spaces in 2028–30 (Sept 30 letter, via dossier §5). Area-wide, 371 of 1,310 spaces were empty at the 2025 peak. School lots did reach 86.8% on one Saturday (utilization CSV), so spare spaces elsewhere don't prove convenient parking at the school (inferred).
- **Our engineer ranked a structure last.** The order was on-street parking, then the middle-school lot, then a structure "only … after other options have been explored." The Feb 4 follow-up says some options "may not be feasible" but names none (traffic memo).
- **Nothing is binding or fully costed.** There's no signed city–school agreement (dossier §2). Free public use is promised in writing only to school-event attendees (Sept 30 letter). There's no operating line in the budget, and the estimate is only option totals ($15.25M for plain concrete in 2026 dollars to $18.11M for brick in 2028 dollars, OAK). A $9M + ~$7M split covers the low option but falls up to ~$2.1M short of the high one (inferred).

**Gates before any bond sale or construction award**
1. **Signed city–school agreement.** It must cover the capital split, a cap on the city's share, school responsibility for its own overruns, the split of operating costs and reserves, enforceable public hours and no-fee public use, and the 3-ft right-of-way.
2. **Published itemized estimate and bids.** Borrow only what the bids require.
3. **Priced alternatives side by side:** a two-level deck, the plain-concrete Option 5, the middle-school lot, and on-street management plus busing. Include a written explanation of which options the Feb 4 letter meant. If a deck is selected, use plain concrete as the cost baseline. Any brick upgrade must be fully funded by the schools within the agreement's allocation of overruns and cap on the city's share.
4. **A funded 20-year operating and reserve budget.** The record supports about $56K–$147K a year. The $56K figure already includes a small sinking fund and is in 2018 dollars. The long-term capital reserve still needs an engineer's estimate.
5. **Confirmed school contribution** and a **costed pedestrian/micromobility project list.**
6. **Exit and queue design** that addresses the 30–60-minute load-out, and **a construction schedule** measured against the 2028–30 crunch.

**Next steps**
- **Before Nov 3**
  - **City Attorney, by Oct 16:** advise on campaign-finance limits for city communications, and on whether a resolution can bind bond issuance and future commissions (to confirm with city attorney).
  - **City Manager and Finance, by Oct 16:** publish neutral facts only: costs, gates, the status of the $7M and the operating range.
  - **Commission, at its next regular meeting (date to confirm with the City Clerk):** adopt the gates by resolution.
- **If it passes**
  - **City Manager with EGRPS, by Jan 31, 2027:** comparison of alternatives, draft agreement, and a student survey.
  - **Commission:** decide size and borrowing amount only after the bids.
- **If it fails**
  - **City Manager with EGRPS, by Dec 15, 2026:** a funded access plan following the engineer's sequence. Any new measure waits until the gates are met, no earlier than May 2027.

**Risks**
- **Cost escalation of about 3.5% a year** (OAK, inferred). Pre-negotiate the agreement now.
- **The deck could miss 2028–30.** The interim plan goes ahead regardless.
- **Students may not use it.** Require the survey and the exit fix.
- **Advocacy limits.** Keep all city material factual.

**What would change this**
- **Toward building as planned:** a signed agreement, bids within budget, and documented reasons the alternatives fail.
- **Toward stopping:** bids above $18.1M, a school share that isn't secured, or no binding public-access terms by bid time.

## Dissents and unresolved disagreements

No model recorded a formal dissent. These differences were narrowed but not erased:
- **What to call it.** Codex calls it a "pause / alternatives first." Claude and Grok call it "proceed with conditions," and argue that "pause" could signal withdrawal while the question is live. The joint text sets the substance (no bond sale or award until the gates are met, with a no-deck option on the table) and avoids both labels in the Decision line.
- **The default size.** Claude would default to a two-level, Option 5 deck unless the schools pay for level 3. Codex objects that the record doesn't establish what level 3 costs at the margin (Schwartz's remark is not a cost allocation), and wants two and three levels priced separately. The joint text requires the comparison and leaves the size to it.
- **"The city's money mainly buys level 3."** Grok and Claude said this in Round 1. Codex says it overstates the evidence, and Grok accepted the correction.
- **Operating cost.** Claude's Round 1 figure of ~$300–330K a year with a reserve rested on secondary Walker benchmarks, and Claude withdrew it in Round 2. All three now treat the reserve as unestimated.
- **The size of the funding gap.** It depends on the option: $9M + ~$7M covers Option 5 in 2026 dollars and falls ~$2.1M short of brick in 2028 dollars (inferred). Claude and Codex place the risk on the schools' side.
- **Dates.** Codex proposed Oct 19 and Oct 26, and Claude proposed "next regular meeting, no later than Oct 19 if it works." The inputs don't give the commission's fall calendar, so the date is to confirm with the City Clerk.

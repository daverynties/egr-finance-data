## Full assessment

*AI analysis by Claude, Oct 9, 2026. It is based on the public record gathered for this guide: city packets, the Progressive Companies traffic memo and follow-up, the La Fave cost emails, the MFCI debt analysis, the 2025 parking study, the superintendent's Sept 30 letter and the comment cards. It is an opinion about what the evidence supports. It is not a recommendation from any official body, and it does not tell anyone how to vote. "Inferred" means arithmetic from cited figures.*

### Bottom line

**The record supports a real but temporary and fixable problem. The ballot asks voters to fund a permanent, larger solution whose key terms aren't written down yet.**

- On the evidence alone, the case for *this* proposal *as presented today* is weaker than the case against it.
- The weakness isn't that the deck is unneeded or unaffordable. It is that:
    1. the main promises (free public use, who maintains it, what the "pedestrian safety" money buys) aren't binding;
    2. the city's own engineer ranked a structure last; and
    3. the city's share mainly buys a third level beyond the school's own need. *(Corrected: In the later panel rounds, Codex argued the record does not show what the third level costs at the margin, so this point overstates the evidence. Claude's conclusion is shown as written.)*
- A voter who puts heavy weight on campus parking during the 2028–2030 construction years, or on a shared Gaslight-area asset, can reasonably vote yes.
- A voter who weighs cost discipline, process or binding terms has solid grounds for no.

**The record as it stands leans against this proposal as written.** Three reasons:

1. **The shortage is temporary.** It's the 2028–2030 construction crunch, not a lasting area-wide gap. In 2025, 371 of 1,310 spaces sat empty at the busiest hour.
2. **A two-level deck covers the school's need.** The city's half mainly buys a third level beyond it.

### The facts that should matter most

1. **The engineer's sequence.** The city's own traffic engineer said to try on-street parking and the middle school lot before building a structure. The follow-up softened this without explaining it.
2. **The third level is the city's purchase.** The school's need is met by two levels. The bond mainly pays for extra public spaces in an area that wasn't full in 2025.
3. **Nothing important is in writing.** Free use, maintenance shares, public hours and the safety projects all depend on an agreement that hasn't been signed.
4. **The yearly bill has no home.** The city's half of upkeep could be $35–165K a year, with no named source. *(Corrected: The $165K end assumed the withdrawn $330K total; half of the panel's $56–147K range is about $28–73K.)*
5. **The tax cost per home is small.** About $100–130 a year for a typical home. Cost alone is not a strong reason to vote no.

### The strongest honest case for YES

- **The construction crunch is real.** Campus parking drops from 216 to about 65 in 2028–2030, and the school says no site exists for another temporary lot.
- **Students on site, off neighborhood streets.** A deck keeps student cars on campus and off residential streets, with no street crossing to park.
- **A shared asset.** It adds lasting shared parking for pool, theater and athletics events and for Gaslight Village, which lost its old structure.
- **The school pays half.** Splitting the cost makes the city's spaces cheaper than building alone. The per-household cost is modest.
- **Accountability.** The commission put it to a vote rather than borrowing without one.
- **Timing.** Waiting means about 3.5% a year in construction escalation, and the next ballot is May 2027.

### The strongest honest case for NO

- **The engineer's advice.** The city's engineer recommended cheaper options first, and no public analysis explains why they were set aside.
- **Size.** The city's money buys a third level beyond the school's need, at roughly $64–77K a space, in an area with spare capacity.
- **No binding terms.** The key promises aren't in any binding document, and voters are approving the money before the terms exist.
- **Upkeep.** Annual costs are unfunded, and the city's figure leaves out long-term repair.
- **Process.** Decisions were made without minutes, ballot language came late, and alternatives were never priced publicly.
- **Timing doesn't fit the need.** With no public schedule *(Corrected: The original said the design was “paused at 50%”; this guide could not confirm that in a primary record, so it is removed here.)*, the deck may not open before the 2028–2030 crunch it's meant to solve.
- **A no can be a "not yet."** A no vote doesn't end the project. The city could return in May 2027 with a signed agreement and itemized costs.

### What would change this assessment

Any of these, made public before or after the vote, would move it toward yes:

1. **A signed city–school agreement** covering cost share, upkeep, public hours and a no-fee commitment for the public.
2. **OAK's itemized estimate**, showing what the $5–8M premium over a plain deck buys.
3. **The cost of a school-only two-level deck**, so the city's increment can be priced honestly.
4. **What "not feasible" meant** in the engineer's Feb 4 letter, and the middle school lot cost.
5. **A construction schedule** showing the deck opens before or during 2028–2030.
6. **A funded line** for the city's share of operations and a repair reserve.
7. **A pedestrian-safety project list** with costs, and crash data for Lake, Bagley and San Lu Rae.

## Its independent city memo, written before seeing the others

**Decision.** Proceed with conditions. The measure stays on the ballot, but before Nov 3 the commission should publish binding terms. If voters approve, no bonds are sold until a signed city–school agreement and real bids show the city's share is justified. Borrow below $9M where the bids allow it.

### Why

- **Need is real but narrow.** The campus drops from 216 to 145 spaces *(Corrected: The original said 144; 216 − 71 = 145.)*, and to about 65 during 2028–30 (research summary §5, Sept 30 letter). But the system peak was 71.7%, leaving 371 empty spaces (utilization CSV). The city's own engineer recommended on-street parking first, then the middle-school lot, and a structure "only… after other options" (traffic memo Nov 10, 2025). A Feb 4 follow-up said some options "may not be feasible" without saying which ones.
- **Costs aren't nailed down.** The deck is $15.2M–$18.1M with only a one-page estimate (Aug 3 packet findings §3). Half of that is $7.6M–$9.1M, while the school has set aside about $7M, so the top estimates leave a gap of up to about $2.1M (inferred). Operations are unfunded: about $147K a year at the Grand Rapids rate (operating-cost PDF, inferred for 240 spaces), up to about $300K–$330K with a repair reserve (inferred). *(Corrected: Claude withdrew the $300–330K figure in Round 2 because it rested on secondary benchmarks; the panel treats the reserve as unestimated.)*
- **Key promises aren't binding.** Free public parking, public hours, street-parking reallocation and the project list are not in any signed document (Aug 3 packet findings §5; research summary §16).

### Conditions or changes required

- Adopt by resolution: free public access hours, how operating costs are split and paid, a capital reserve, and no bond sale without a signed agreement (binding effect to confirm with city attorney).
- Publish the itemized cost estimate, a 20-year operating budget, and the pedestrian and micromobility project list.
- Cap the city share at the plain-concrete design (Option 5) unless the school pays the cost of brick (Aug 3 packet findings §3).

### Next steps

- *Before Nov 3:* City Manager posts an even-handed, factual summary by Oct 16. City Attorney advises by Oct 13 on limits to advocacy with public resources. Commission votes on the terms resolution at its next regular meeting (date to confirm).
- *If it passes:* City Manager and Superintendent bring a signed agreement by Feb 2027. Finance sizes the bonds to the bids. Staff fund a student survey and an exit-design fix before construction documents are finished.
- *If it fails:* Staff and the district bring a plan for on-street management, the middle-school lot (up to 103 spaces) and the busing pilot by Jan 2027. Any revised measure waits until May 2027 at the earliest (Aug 3 packet findings §1).

### Risks and how to manage them

- **Escalation of about 3.5% a year** (Aug 3 packet findings §3): rebid with a firm cap on cost.
- **Low student use, with a 30–60 minute exit** (traffic memo): run the survey and fix the exit design.
- **The deck can't open before the 2028–30 crunch** (research summary §5, inferred): run the interim busing and on-street plan either way.
- **Reserves can't cover the city's share:** $6.5M is less than $7.6M (fact-check of this guide).
- **Advocacy rules:** stay factual and keep campaigning out of city channels.
- **Data quality:** some inputs are dated Oct 9 and several figures are secondary-only. Verify them against the primary records.

### What would change the decision

- **Toward pausing:** the district can't confirm its full half; bids come in above $18.1M; the district won't sign binding public-access terms; or the survey shows students won't use the deck.
- **Toward proceeding as planned:** a signed agreement, an itemized estimate within budget, and evidence that the alternatives really are infeasible.

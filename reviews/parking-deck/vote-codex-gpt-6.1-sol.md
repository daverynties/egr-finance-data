**1. Vote: No — 80% confidence, based on this record.**

The documents establish a real loss of school parking and a potential benefit from keeping students on campus. They do **not** establish that a roughly 240-space deck is the best response at the proposed financial commitment.

That confidence describes my recommendation given the supplied evidence, not the probability that the project would succeed. **2. Top three reasons**

1. **The counts do not establish a broad parking shortage.** Across 29 observations on three dates, total occupancy peaked at **939 of 1,310 spaces, or 71.68%**, leaving 371 vacant. Public-city parking never exceeded **65.70%** occupancy. These aggregate figures cannot establish availability near a particular entrance, but they weaken the case that hundreds of additional spaces are necessary. [Utilization CSV, lines 11–25](/workspace/parking-vote/codex-in/docs/parking-deck-utilization-2025.csv:11)

2. **The engineer recommended trying other options first, and the subsequent qualification remains unresolved.** The November memo recommended proceeding without parking improvements, considering a bus pilot, then expanding the middle-school lot if problems emerged, and considering a deck after other options. The February follow-up says some options may be infeasible but identifies neither the options nor their specific limitations. That leaves a material gap in the justification for choosing the deck. [Traffic memo, PDF pp. 3–4/images 3–4](/workspace/parking-vote/codex-in/docs/page-images/parking-deck-traffic-memo-2025-11-10-3.png), [February follow-up, PDF p. 1/image 1](/workspace/parking-vote/codex-in/docs/page-images/parking-deck-traffic-memo-2025-11-10-1.png)

3. **The commitment is substantial relative to the demonstrated need, with important obligations unspecified.** The city’s projected repayment is **$13,212,287**, including **$4,212,287 interest**. Under the older site plan, a 240-space deck would provide approximately **120 net additional school-system spaces** after accounting for replacements and losses. Operating responsibilities, capital reserves, public access and a confirmed total project budget are missing. [Debt schedule, lines 2–21](/workspace/parking-vote/codex-in/docs/parking-deck-bond-9M-debt-schedule.csv:2), [Traffic memo, PDF p. 2/image 2](/workspace/parking-vote/codex-in/docs/page-images/parking-deck-traffic-memo-2025-11-10-2.png), [Parking map, PDF p. 5/image 5](/workspace/parking-vote/codex-in/docs/page-images/parking-deck-traffic-memo-2025-11-10-5.png)

**3. What would change my vote**

I would switch to Yes if additional primary documents established all three:

- **A recurring, consequential shortage:** location-specific counts covering school arrivals, dismissal, evening activities and major events, with projections after the expansion.
- **A persuasive comparison of alternatives:** a revised engineering analysis explaining which alternatives fail, their costs, and why this deck produces better overall safety and access after accounting for queues and pedestrian conflicts.
- **A complete financial and operating commitment:** a verified project budget and final space count, plus a signed city–school agreement covering overruns, operations, major repairs, reserves and enforceable public access.

A deck could be justified by dependable campus access and reduced street crossings even with aggregate vacancies. Those benefits need to be measured against the alternatives and costs.

## 1. Progressive Companies traffic memo (`parking-deck-traffic-memo-2025-11-10.pdf`, 6 pages)

The PDF holds two letters and two maps.

### Feb 4, 2026 follow-up letter (page 1)
To Doug La Fave from Joseph P. Eberle, PE, Transportation Engineer:

> "This letter is meant to address the previous memo submitted on 11/10/2025 regarding the East Grand Rapids High School Parking, specifically the loss of the senior parking lot on the north side of Lake Drive and how that will affect school traffic.
>
> In that memo, several parking options were discussed and recommended with respect to the new high school expansion. After additional consultation with the City and schools, the schools review noted that some options and recommendations may not be feasible due to certain on-campus limitations and variables that are present. As the city and schools continue to evaluate potential parking options, on-campus and the surrounding external city road/pedestrian network factors should be evaluated related to vehicle, pedestrian, and mobility user impacts to ensure that the best solution is implemented."

**The follow-up doesn't say which options are infeasible, and it doesn't withdraw the recommendation in so many words.** That same day (Feb 4, 2:50 pm), La Fave emailed the Shared Parking Manual cost page to Anthony Morey (see §2).

### Nov 10, 2025 memo (pages 2–4)
- **The problem:** the expansion takes the senior lot (Lot 15, 111 spaces, used mainly by students). The site plan keeps 40 spaces, "resulting in a net loss of 71 parking spaces."
- **On-street parking:** a study of side streets between Lake Drive and Argentina Drive "showed an average capacity of 111 parking spaces and a minimum of 89 parking spaces during a school day." These "should provide a sufficient amount of parking to accommodate the 71 lost parking spaces."
  - Drawbacks: a longer walk, crossing Lake Drive, and possible resident complaints, "however there are not any known complaints about the current students using this parking."
  - Lake Drive "has several signed and marked crosswalks on each side street."
- **Middle school lot:** expansion "anticipated to add up to 103 parking spaces," replacing the tennis courts. It is about 1,000 ft from the high school, with a crossing of Lakeside Drive (signalized crosswalk at Lake Dr plus an RRFB by the library). That lot is "full during school hours, emptying after school, and then filling to capacity again in the evening."
- **Parking structure:** "A two (2) to four (4) level parking structure ... in the current location of Lot 11 ... is proposed to have 136–291 parking spaces and will serve to replace the parking spaces in Lots 11 and 15."
  - "Students that park at this location will not be required to cross any streets, however, they may be required to walk around the High School to get to the new main entrance that will be constructed where Lot 15 is located (approximately 1,000 feet)."
  - "Bagley Avenue will likely experience traffic backing up in the morning before school. Exiting traffic after school will be anticipated to cause less network traffic issues; however, load out of the structure is anticipated to take 30 minutes to one (1) hour ... These delays can result in less desire to park in the parking structure with students and staff instead choosing to utilize existing on-street parking."
- **Bussing pilot:** buses from elementary schools or parks for students who live far away, run during construction and kept if popular.
- **Recommendations:**
  - "Progressive Companies recommends constructing the High School expansion without any parking improvements. Several parking studies show that sufficient on-street parking exists within 1,500 feet of the High School and less accommodating parking will encourage more carpooling and walking/biking to school."
  - If problems occur afterward, the middle school lot expansion comes next, "at a less expensive price than a parking structure."
  - "If the Middle School parking lot expansion is constructed and parking problems still occur, then further consideration can be given to a parking structure on campus. Due to the cost and complexity of operating a parking structure, this option should only be considered after other options have been explored."
- **Not in the memo:** level of service (LOS) grades and the phrase "LOS F". There is no intersection capacity analysis.

## 2. "Operating cost" file (`parking-deck-operating-cost-2026.pdf`, 4 pages)

**This is not a city breakdown of the $160K.** It holds two emails and a textbook page.

### Doug La Fave email, Apr 23, 2026, to Shea Charles, Anthony Morey and Heidi Kattula
> "She advised that the operations and maintenance (not depreciation) costs per parking spot for parking decks is $611 per space per year. For the three-story deck that is being considered, that would be $130,143 per year total. They increase that per spot cost for projections each year by CPI."

- "She" is Jessica Smith, Director of Mobile GR, City of Grand Rapids.
- **Derived:** $130,143 ÷ $611 = 213 spaces. So the city's number was for the April 213-space design. At 240 spaces it is **$146,640** (inferred).

### Doug La Fave email, Feb 4, 2026, to Anthony Morey
It sends the Shared Parking Manual (3rd ed., NPA/ICSC/ULI) section on the cost to own and operate parking ramps, "adjusting for the difference via CPI."

### Shared Parking Manual, Figure 5-1 (2018 dollars)
- **Typical above-grade structure with parking ramps:**
  - Construction: $12,500–$22,500 per space
  - Cost to own and operate, free parking with no security: **$140–$240 per space per month**
  - Paid parking with security: $180–$280
- **Assumptions:**
  - Project cost = construction + 10% soft costs + 15% financing
  - 7% interest, 20-year loan
- **Operating costs:**
  - Basic above-grade: **$175 per space per year** (utilities, maintenance, insurance)
  - **Sinking fund** for future capital maintenance (including elevators and PARCS): **$60 per space per year** above grade
  - Revenue controls (PARCS): $375 per space per year
  - Security: median $125 per space per year

**Derived (inferred):**
- **Basic O&M plus sinking fund:** ($175 + $60) × 240 = **$56,400 a year in 2018 dollars**, roughly **$70K–$75K today** after CPI of about +25–33%.
- **Range:** the city's own two sources run from about $56K to $147K a year before any capital reserve. The GR figure covers decks that Mobile GR staffs and charges for. An unstaffed free deck is closer to the Manual's basic figure.

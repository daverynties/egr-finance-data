# Joint recommendation — East Grand Rapids parking-deck bond (Nov 3, 2026)
*Grok, Claude and Codex acting as hypothetical city leaders. This is an AI exercise, not an official city position. Facts come from the input record; "(inferred)" marks our own arithmetic or judgment; "(to confirm with city attorney)" marks legal mechanics the record doesn't establish.*

**Decision.** Let the certified Nov 3 question go to voters. Make no city commitment yet: no bond sale, no construction award and no signed cost-share until a published set of gates is met. One gate is a priced comparison with smaller and cheaper options. If the gates are met, build at the size and cost the comparison supports, with borrowing sized to the bids. Separately, deliver a 2028–30 student-access plan whatever the vote.

**Why**
- **Real but narrow need.** The expansion removes 71 spaces (traffic memo, Nov 10, 2025), and the campus drops to about 65 spaces in 2028–30 (Sept 30 letter, via dossier §5). Area-wide, 371 of 1,310 spaces were empty at the 2025 peak. School lots did reach 86.8% on one Saturday (utilization CSV), so spare spaces elsewhere don't prove convenient parking at the school (inferred).
- **Our engineer ranked a structure last.** The order was on-street parking, then the middle-school lot, then a structure "only … after other options have been explored." The Feb 4 follow-up says some options "may not be feasible" but names none (traffic memo).
- **Nothing is binding or fully costed.** There's no signed city–school agreement (dossier §2). Free public use is promised in writing only to school-event attendees (Sept 30 letter). There's no operating line in the budget, and the estimate is only option totals ($15.25M–$18.11M, OAK). A $9M + ~$7M split covers the low option but falls up to ~$2.1M short of the high one (inferred).

**Gates before any bond sale or construction award**
1. **Signed city–school agreement.** It must cover the capital split, a cap on the city's share, school responsibility for its own overruns, the split of operating costs and reserves, enforceable public hours and no-fee public use, and the 3-ft right-of-way.
2. **Published itemized estimate and bids.** Borrow only what the bids require.
3. **Priced alternatives side by side:** a two-level deck, the plain-concrete Option 5, the middle-school lot, and on-street management plus busing. Include a written explanation of which options the Feb 4 letter meant. The city pays for Option 5 unless the schools fund the brick upgrade.
4. **A funded 20-year operating and reserve budget.** The record supports about $56K–$147K a year before any capital reserve. The reserve still needs an engineer's estimate.
5. **Confirmed school contribution** and a **costed pedestrian/micromobility project list.**
6. **Exit and queue design** that addresses the 30–60-minute load-out, and **a construction schedule** measured against the 2028–30 crunch.

**Next steps**
- **Before Nov 3**
  - **City Attorney, by Oct 16:** advise on campaign-finance limits for city communications, and on whether a resolution can bind bond issuance and future commissions (to confirm with city attorney).
  - **City Manager and Finance, by Oct 16:** publish neutral facts only: costs, gates, the status of the $7M and the operating range.
  - **Commission, at its next regular meeting (date to confirm with the City Clerk):** adopt the gates by resolution.
- **If it passes**
  - **City Manager with EGRPS, by Jan 31, 2027:** comparison of alternatives, draft agreement, and a student survey.
  - **Commission:** decide size and borrowing amount only after the bids.
- **If it fails**
  - **City Manager with EGRPS, by Dec 15, 2026:** a funded access plan following the engineer's sequence. Any new measure waits until the gates are met, no earlier than May 2027.

**Risks**
- **Cost escalation of about 3.5% a year** (OAK, inferred). Pre-negotiate the agreement now.
- **The deck could miss 2028–30.** The interim plan goes ahead regardless.
- **Students may not use it.** Require the survey and the exit fix.
- **Advocacy limits.** Keep all city material factual.

**What would change this**
- **Toward building as planned:** a signed agreement, bids within budget, and documented reasons the alternatives fail.
- **Toward stopping:** bids above $18.1M, a school share that isn't secured, or no binding public-access terms by bid time.

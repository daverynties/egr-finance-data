# Due Diligence: East Grand Rapids Public Parking Facility and Pedestrian Safety Bond Proposal

**$9M, November 3, 2026 ballot | 240-space deck on Bagley at EGRHS | City/schools 50/50**
Independent analysis compiled October 8, 2026. Every number cites a primary source. Figures marked "(inferred)" are the author's arithmetic from cited inputs, not stated in any source.

---

## Executive Summary

**What voters are being asked:** Authorize up to $9 million in unlimited-tax general obligation bonds (20-year max per series) for the city's half of a 240-space, three-level parking deck on Bagley Street at East Grand Rapids High School, plus traffic signals and pedestrian/micromobility infrastructure. The school district would fund its half (~$7M) from existing funds. Total project cost: $15.2M–$18.1M depending on design and bid year.

**The core tension:** The city's own traffic engineer recommended building the high school expansion *without any parking improvements*, finding sufficient on-street parking within 1,500 feet. The 2025 parking study found system-wide occupancy peaking at 72% on weekdays (school lots hit 87% on one Saturday). The deck would add a net ~190 spaces versus the no-deck future — at $80K–$95K per net space (inferred). Annual operating costs (~$147K at Grand Rapids benchmarks, inferred for 240 spaces) plus capital reserves (~$152K–$181K/yr, inferred) would run roughly double the city's stated $160K figure, which excludes depreciation. No intergovernmental agreement has been signed. The commission placed the measure on the ballot 4–1–1 after a failed postponement motion; one commissioner walked out.

**Bottom line for voters:** The need case rests on a projected future shortage (campus drops to ~65 spaces during 2028–2030 construction), not current system-wide overcrowding. The cost case depends on which design is built and whether bids come in below estimates. The governance case has gaps: no signed cost-sharing agreement, no binding commitment to remove street parking (the premise of the bike-lane benefit), and no dedicated funding for ongoing maintenance.

## 1. Need: Does the Data Show a Parking Shortage?

### Current occupancy (2025 combined parking study)

Source: June 29, 2026 commission work session packet; full hourly data in `parking-deck-utilization-2025.csv`.

| Date | System peak | School peak | Public/city peak |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mar 12, 2025 (Wed) | 71.7% (939 of 1,310) | 81.0% | 65.7% |
| May 8, 2025 (Thu) | data in CSV | — | — |
| May 10, 2025 (Sat) | — | **86.8% at 1pm** | — |

371 spaces sat empty at the absolute system peak. The 85% benchmark (industry standard for "tight supply") was not reached system-wide on any study date. **Correction:** an earlier version of this analysis stated no category hit 85%. The Saturday data shows school lots at 86.8% and 85.4% — above the benchmark on weekends. (Claude's dossier was correct.)

### The 72-space shortfall

Source: Progressive Companies traffic memo, Nov 10, 2025 (posted by Safe Streets EGR).

The senior lot (Lot 15) has 111 spaces. The expansion keeps 40, for a **net loss of 71 spaces**. This is the originating problem — a planning outcome of the $158.9M school bond project, not a growth-driven shortage.

### Campus space counts over time

Source: April 2026 city memo; Sept 30, 2026 superintendent letter (via Claude's dossier).

| When | Campus spaces |
|---|---|
| Before construction | 216 |
| Fall 2026 (now) | 77 + temporary lot ~150 |
| Phase 2 (2028–2030) | **~65** (no temporary lot possible) |
| Post-construction, no deck | 144 |
| With 213-space deck | 308 |
| With 240-space deck | ~334 (inferred) |

**Net gain vs. the no-deck future: ~190 spaces** (inferred: 334 − 144). The deck replaces the ~50-space Bagley surface lot (Area 11 in the study).

### What the traffic engineer actually recommended

Source: Progressive Companies memo, Nov 10, 2025, "Recommendations" section.

> "Progressive Companies recommends constructing the High School expansion **without any parking improvements**. Several parking studies show that sufficient on-street parking exists within 1,500 feet of the High School."

The memo further recommended a middle school lot expansion (up to 103 spaces, cheaper) *if* problems materialize, and said a parking structure "should only be considered after other options have been explored" due to "cost and complexity of operating."

**The city and schools went directly to the option the engineer ranked last.**

The memo also warned that the deck's 30-to-60-minute exit would cause "less desire to park in the parking structure with students and staff instead choosing to utilize existing on-street parking" — directly undercutting the utilization assumption.

### Assessment

The need is real but narrow and time-bound: a projected ~65-space campus during 2028–2030 construction, and a permanent 71-space deficit versus today. It is not a current system-wide shortage — 371 empty spaces at peak proves that. Whether 190 net new spaces at $80K–$95K each (inferred) is the right solution to a 71-space problem is the central question.

## 2. Cost

### Construction

Source: OAK executive summary in Aug 3, 2026 packet (via Claude's dossier and packet findings).

| Design | 2026 dollars | 2028 dollars |
|---|---|---|
| Option 5 (utilitarian, exposed concrete) | $15,246,696 | $16,332,641 |
| Option 1 (brick facade) | $16,905,675 | $18,109,782 |

The packet contains totals only — **no line items were released**, even to the commission.

**Cost per net new space** (inferred, vs. 190 net spaces):
- Utilitarian: ~$80K–$86K
- Brick: ~$89K–$95K

**Design premium** (inferred): ~$1.66M in 2026 dollars for brick over utilitarian, or ~$830K per partner.

**Industry comparison:** Walker Consultants' 2026 Michigan benchmark is ~$35K/space construction, ~$42K/space all-in for a plain deck. EGR's $63K–$75K/space (inferred, gross) reflects the architectural treatment, heated sidewalks, snow-melt, elevator, and 368.8 sq ft/space efficiency (vs. ~300 industry standard). The premium over a plain deck is roughly $5M–$8M (inferred).

**Cost history:** OAK's August 2024 basis was $10.5M. April 2026: $13.6M for 213 spaces. July 2026: ~$14M referenced. August 2026: $15.2M–$18.1M. Costs have risen ~50–70% in two years.

### Operating and maintenance

Source: Doug La Fave email, April 23, 2026 (posted by Safe Streets EGR); Shared Parking Manual 3rd ed., Fig. 5-1; Walker Consultants 2026.

| Component | Annual (240 spaces) | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Operations & maintenance (no depreciation) | ~$147K (inferred: $611 × 240) | Grand Rapids Mobile GR via La Fave |
| Capital repair reserve | ~$152K–$181K (inferred: 1% of construction cost) | Walker benchmark |
| **Full annual cost** | **~$300K–$330K** (inferred) | |
| **City's half** | **~$150K–$165K/yr** (inferred) | 50/50 split |

The city's stated ~$160K figure matches the operating line alone and **excludes capital reserves**. Lifecycle items from the July 22 design deck: joint sealants (7–10 yrs), traffic membrane (7–15 yrs), snow-melt boilers (10–15 yrs), elevator modernization (15–20 yrs).

**No dedicated funding source for the city's annual share has been identified.** It would come from the general fund or a future millage.

### Architect fees

Source: Pure Architects proposal, March 20, 2026 (via Claude's dossier).

Total base fees: **$640,332** plus $22,389 reimbursables — for schematic through construction administration. Excludes surveys, geotechnical, traffic, environmental, and cost-estimating consultants. Design is paused at 50% design development.

## 3. Financing and Tax Impact

### Debt service

Source: MFCI analysis, July 30, 2026, in Aug 3 packet; full schedule in `parking-deck-bond-9M-debt-schedule.csv`.

| | |
|---|---|
| Principal | $9,000,000 |
| Total interest | $4,212,282 |
| **Total debt service** | **$13,212,282** |
| True interest cost | 4.13% |
| Year-1 millage | 0.5922 |
| Average millage | 0.4925 |
| Avg. annual debt service | ~$660K |

Assumes 2% annual taxable value growth on a $1.097B base. If growth is slower, the millage runs higher.

**Rate history:** 3.33% (April) → 3.91% (July) → 4.13% (adopted). Rising rates added roughly $2M in total cost versus the April concept.

### Household cost (inferred)

| Home | Taxable value (approx.) | Avg./year | Year 1 | 20-year total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $300K market (newer purchase) | ~$150K | ~$74 | ~$89 | ~$1,480 |
| $521K median (Census) | ~$200–260K | ~$100–$128 | ~$120–$154 | ~$2,000–$2,570 |
| Per $100K taxable | $100K | $49 | $59 | $985 |

Per-household share of total debt: ~$3,480 over 20 years (inferred: $13.2M ÷ 3,797 households). Businesses and non-homestead property also pay.

### City finances

Source: FY2025 ACFR; Kent County apportionment report (via Claude's dossier).

- Unassigned general fund balance: **$6.5M (44% of expenditures)** — well above the 20–25% policy target. The city *could* fund its share without borrowing, but would draw reserves below target.
- Existing governmental debt: $10.7M. Net pension liability: $5.4M. OPEB: $3.3M.
- **Correction:** an earlier file stated EGR has the highest city levy of six nearby cities at 14.21 mills. The 2024 apportionment report shows EGR at 14.09 mills — third-highest in the wider county after Cedar Springs (18.77) and Lowell (15.95), but highest among the six-city comparison set originally used (Grand Rapids, Kentwood, Wyoming, Walker, Grandville, EGR). The 14.21 figure is likely the 2025 levy; unconfirmed. (Claude's dossier was correct to flag this.)

## 4. Governance and Process

### Vote record

Source: Official minutes (in Aug 17 packet); April minutes (in May 4 packet). Via Claude's dossier.

| Date | Motion | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Apr 20, 2026 | 2026-53-A: up to $100K for deck design | **5–2** (No: Groff-Blaszak, Hunter) |
| Apr 20, 2026 | 2026-53-B: reimbursement resolution | 7–0 |
| Aug 3, 2026 | 2026-150-A: postpone to special meeting | **Failed 2–4** (Yes: Hunter, Groff-Blaszak) |
| Aug 3, 2026 | 2026-150-B: place $9M on ballot | **4–1–1** (No: Wessely; Abstain: Groff-Blaszak) |
| Aug 6, 2026 | 2026-147: fix millage typo | **4–1–2** (No: Wessely; Abstain: Hunter, Groff-Blaszak) |

**Correction:** an earlier file described the ballot vote as "4-1-2." That was the Aug 6 typo-fix vote. The Aug 3 vote to place the bond on the ballot was **4–1–1**. (Claude's dossier was correct.)

Commissioner Hunter left the Aug 3 meeting at 8:23 PM after the postponement failed. On Aug 6 he abstained citing his profession (financial advisor) as a possible conflict; the nature of the conflict is not documented.

### Process concerns

- **Subcommittee opacity:** The joint parking subcommittee (Burdick, Wessely, Schwartz) met below quorum, so the Open Meetings Act did not apply and **no minutes exist**. The body that shaped the project left no record. (East Insider)
- **Late ballot language:** The 33-page revised resolution arrived 90 minutes before the Aug 3 meeting and was not public beforehand. (East Insider)
- **Pedestrian-safety language** was added to the ballot in August 2026 — late in the process. No pedestrian/micromobility project list or budget exists. (Claude's dossier)
- **No signed agreement:** As of the analysis date, no intergovernmental agreement on cost-sharing, operations, or maintenance has been signed. (FOX 17, Aug 4)
- **Commission chose the ballot over bypass:** On July 20, the commission considered non-voter-approved LTGO bonds (repaid from general fund, no public vote) and chose to seek voter approval instead. Credit where due.

### The third level

The April concept was ~213 spaces; the July design is 240. The third level is the city's incremental contribution — it converts a school replacement project into a shared public facility. Without the city's participation, the school would build a smaller deck (or nothing) for its own 71-space deficit.

### Gaslight history

Source: 2004 Jade Pig PUD; revised PUD memo; court records (via Claude's dossier).

The original Gaslight PUD required the parking structure to be "retained and maintained" (687 spaces required) and available for high school events. The structure was removed in January 2025. On Aug 10, 2026, Judge Quist voided the PUD amendment approval. The city states the old deck "was never more than 50% utilized during peak periods."

## 7. Risks and Open Questions

**What's not binding:**
1. No signed city–school agreement (cost share, operations, maintenance, public hours, fees)
2. No commitment to remove street parking (the premise of the bike-lane benefit)
3. No commitment to keep parking free (April memo vs. Sept 30 letter differ in scope)
4. Final design, space count, and facade choice are all undecided
5. The $9M is a cap; actual borrowing follows bids — but so does the final project scope

**What could go over budget:**
1. Construction escalation: $15.2M (2026) → $18.1M (2028) at ~3.5%/yr. Every year of delay adds ~$500K+
2. The signal: $500K placeholder; WalkSafeEGR cites up to $600K
3. Extras flagged "to be determined": truck loading dock reconfiguration, snow-melt systems, utility work
4. Operating costs exclude capital reserves; full cost ~$300K–$330K/yr (inferred) vs. $160K stated
5. Interest rates rose from 3.33% to 4.13% in four months; further rises increase debt service

**What's unknown:**
1. Whether students will use the deck (no survey; engineer predicts many won't due to exit delays)
2. Whether the deck can open before the 2028–2030 crunch (design paused at 50%; no schedule published)
3. Who funds the city's ~$75K–$85K/yr operating share and future capital repairs
4. What the $7M school set-aside's formal status is (board motion from general/capital funds; not voter-restricted)
5. Crash history at Lake/Bagley/San Lu Rae (no published data — the empirical basis for the "pedestrian safety" framing)

**Unreleased records** (20 items catalogued in Claude's dossier §16, including subcommittee communications, OAK line-item estimates, comment cards, the draft agreement, and the full 2025 study narrative). Their absence is itself a finding.

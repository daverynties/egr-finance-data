# East Grand Rapids Parking Deck Bond — Full Context
Compiled Oct 8, 2026. For independent analysis.

## The proposal

**Ballot name:** "Public Parking Facility and Pedestrian Safety Bond Proposal"
**Election:** Nov 3, 2026
**Amount:** Up to $9,000,000 in bonds, maximum 20-year term per series
**Millage:** 0.5922 mills estimated first year; 0.4925-mill simple average over bond life
**Cost per household:** ~$148/year average on $300K taxable value (~$2,960 over 20 years); ~$49/year per $100K taxable value
**Total debt service:** ~$13,212,282 (per city's bond analysis)

**What it builds:**
- 240-space, three-level public parking deck on Bagley at East Grand Rapids High School site
- Traffic signals at Lake Drive and at Bagley/San Lu Rae (~$500,000 placeholder)
- Pedestrian and micromobility safety infrastructure

**Partnership structure:** 50/50 city/schools. City borrows up to $9M (its share). Schools report $7M available from prior millages for their share. Total project cost estimated $15-18M (Fishbeck and Owen-Ames-Kimball design work, subcontractor pricing). City will wait for construction bids before sizing the bond — may borrow less than $9M.

**Not in the bond:** ~$160,000/year in ongoing deck maintenance and operations (from city budget, no dedicated source identified). Elevator maintenance. Traffic light operating costs.

## Origin and timeline

- **2023:** Voters approve $158.9M school bond (77.5% yes) for high school transformation.
- **2024-2025:** During final planning, schools discover the new design has 71-72 fewer parking spaces than the existing senior lot. Underground parking considered, rejected on cost.
- **May 2025:** City agrees to join school officials in a joint parking subcommittee.
- **May-July 2026:** Three community engagement sessions at EGRHS (May 27, June 23, July 22).
- **June 2026:** Progressive Engineering (Chris Saul, Suzanne Schultz) presents citywide parking study at commission work session.
- **Aug 3, 2026:** Commission votes to place bond on Nov 3 ballot.
- **Aug 2026:** Revised ballot language adopted 4-1-2, adding pedestrian-safety and micromobility language.
- **Sept 30, 2026:** Superintendent Heidi Kattula sends letter to families with project update.

## The parking study (June 2026, Progressive Engineering)

- No part of the system exceeded the 85% occupancy benchmark used to signal tight supply.
- Finding uses system-wide averages across on-street and off-street spaces, not peak event nights in Gaslight Village.
- A deck space requires ~300 sq ft vs ~150-175 for on-street. Industry cost ~$60,000 per space.
- Traffic engineer: students in the deck "will not be required to cross any streets" (vs crossing Lake Drive for on-street).
- Traffic engineer warned: morning backups on Bagley likely; 30-60 minute after-school exit with single entrance — final exit design matters.

## Alternatives the schools evaluated

Per Aug 4, 2026 commission meeting (city manager presentation):
- Two-story deck
- Underground parking (rejected: cost)
- Track-top parking
- Senior lot expansion
- The deck would encroach 3 feet into city right-of-way (requires intergovernmental agreement).

## Supporter arguments

Sources: safestreetsegr.com, commission meetings, school district communications.

1. Consolidates scattered surface lots, reduces surface parking footprint, frees land for other uses.
2. Student safety: no street crossing required.
3. Shifting parking into the deck allows reallocation of street parking to dedicated bike/e-bike/scooter lanes (Wealthy between Lovett and Lakeside currently has only sharrows because bike plan was implemented without removing parking).
4. Good planning for a land-locked, built-out community.
5. 240 spaces near Gaslight Village improves access for events and businesses.
6. City only borrows what bids require — may be less than $9M.

## Critic arguments

Sources: EGR Residents' Community Group posts (Sept-Oct 2026), opposition documents, resident texts.

1. **Incomplete cost picture:** $9M covers construction only. ~$160K/year maintenance, elevator upkeep, and signal operating costs have no dedicated funding — likely future budget pressure or future millages. ("Yet another forever east tax.")
2. **Accountability gap:** The 71-72 space deficit was a planning error in the $158.9M school bond project. No one has been held accountable; taxpayers are asked to cover it.
3. **Utilization doubt:** Students won't use it — they won't wait 30-40 minutes to exit, and they want quick off-campus lunch access. If students don't use it, the deck doesn't solve the stated problem.
4. **Street parking won't go away:** No binding commitment to remove street parking after the deck opens. Without that, the walkability and bike-lane benefits don't materialize.
5. **Monetization risk:** A public deck will face pressure to charge for event parking (football games, Gaslight events). "Free and unstaffed" has no enforcement mechanism against future commissions.
6. **Developer history:** The Gaslight PUD developer tore down the prior deck, restricted property to private parking, and walked away from original commitments. Taxpayers are backfilling a private developer's obligations.
7. **Tax burden stacking:** EGR already has the highest city levy of six nearby cities (14.21 mills). Each new millage compounds the affordability question for fixed-income and moderate-income residents.
8. **Per-space cost:** At ~$12M total taxpayer cost over 20 years for the city's share, critics calculate ~$170,000 per space for the 72-space deficit that started this.
9. **Process:** Pedestrian-safety language was added late (August 2026). Some residents view the ballot timing as rushed.

## What the records don't establish

- Whether the city will actually remove street parking after the deck opens (no binding commitment found).
- Whether future commissions will keep the deck free and unstaffed (no enforcement mechanism found).
- What happened to the earlier underground garage concept (no public record found).
- Whether the $7M in school funds is formally earmarked or could be reallocated.
- The final exit/entrance design and whether it addresses the 30-60 minute exit concern.

## Studies

### 2017 EGR Parking Study (city-commissioned)
Source: eastgrmi.gov DocumentCenter View/1336 (Master Plan Initial Concepts, Nov 14, 2017)

Saturday Oct 21, 2017 utilization counts, Gaslight Village:
- On-street: 45% (morning) to 78% (lunch), 51-74% other times
- Off-street: 23% to 56%
- Total system: 29% to 59%

Key findings (city's own study):
- "Parking capacity in Gaslight Village is much greater than the demand"
- On-street fills faster than off-street; core (Wealthy and Bagley) most utilized
- Lunch/afternoon highest demand
- Two largest private facilities consistently underutilized
- "Structure consistently less than half occupied"
- "Cars currently using structure can be accommodated without [it] — would push total system utilization to 87% at peak demand"

Management recommendations included: shared parking authority, valet services, restriping for more on-street spaces, time-limit enforcement, investing in alternative transportation.

### 2026 Progressive Engineering parking study
Presented June 2026 at commission work session. Consultants: Chris Saul, Suzanne Schultz.

- No part of the system exceeded the 85% occupancy benchmark at any point.
- Analysis included on-street and publicly available off-street lots.
- System-wide averages, not peak event nights.
- Deck space: ~300 sq ft per space vs ~150-175 for on-street.
- Industry cost: ~$60,000 per deck space.

Note: The full study document was not found in public search. The findings above are from Citizen Portal AI summaries of the work session. The study itself may be in commission packet archives.

### Fishbeck / Owen-Ames-Kimball design work
Referenced in Aug 4, 2026 commission meeting. Source of the $15-18M cost range.
- Low-end 2026 construction estimate: ~$15M
- High-end (later bid years): just over $18M
- The full design documents were not found in public search.

## City commission meeting findings

### June 29, 2026 work session (51-page packet)
Topic: "Work session to review the parking system in EGR."
- Included the Combined Parking Study 2025 with utilization charts for 3/12/25 (weekday), 5/8/25 (weekday), 5/10/25 (weekend), broken out by school vs public/city parking.
- The full data tables are in the packet charts (not extracted here).

### Aug 3, 2026 commission meeting (293-page packet)
City Manager Shea Charles memo — key points:

**Design (Pure Architects + Fishbeck, since May 2026):**
- ~240 spaces (initial target was 220; top level extended to north wall)
- Designated drop-off lane on south side
- 10-foot pedestrian path along north property line
- At-grade pedestrian entrances at three corners
- Stair tower + elevator at southwest corner (near PAC); stair tower at northwest corner
- Heated sidewalks around the deck
- Top level may serve as snow storage in winter
- Five architectural concepts: Option 1 (brick facades, most expensive) through Option 5 (utilitarian, exposed concrete, cheapest)
- Number of spaces can be reduced as project proceeds

**Costs (O.A.K. estimates):**
- Option 1: $16,905,675 (2026 dollars)
- Option 5: $15,246,696 (2026 dollars)
- 2028 projections: Option 1 $18,109,782; Option 5 $16,332,641
- Memo asked commission to consider up to $10M (Option 1 in 2028 dollars + ~$1M contingency)
- MFCI debt analysis at $10M: average annual debt service $733,843; year-one millage 0.6570
- Per $1M borrowed: $73,384/year debt service
- Actual borrowing to be determined after bids received

**Community feedback:**
- Three sessions (May, June, July), 30-40 attendees each
- "A few comments of support... with mostly opposition expressed, especially those who live in close proximity"
- GVBA, adjacent building owners, Bagley Condo owners met separately
- Building owners: concerned about losing vehicle access through the lot (deliveries, parking, waste hauling)
- Bagley Condo owners: safety, traffic, loading/unloading time, environmental impact

**Commission options presented:**
1. Adopt bond resolution at $10M or lower
2. Defer and request more information
3. Use non-voter-approved bonds (different resolution)
4. Develop other funding options
5. Don't proceed

Note: Ballot ultimately set at $9M (not $10M) — commission lowered the ask.

### Aug 6, 2026 special meeting (8-page packet)
Purpose: correct a technical error in the Aug 3 bond resolution.
- The resolution stated 0.44925 mills instead of 0.4925 mills (average annual rate).
- Corrected resolution for $9M confirmed.

### Notable from the corrected resolution (Aug 6)
The WHEREAS clauses state the city's rationale:
- "Shifting a portion of existing city parking capacity to the proposed public parking garage would allow some street surface parking space to be reallocated to further the city's mobility and pedestrian safety goals, including placement of dedicated travel lanes"
- Construction "will further the City's goals of reducing the need for on-street and surface parking, supporting the objectives of the City's Mobility Plan, and reducing reliance on privately owned parking"
- Traffic signal at Bagley/San Lu Rae and Lake "expected to facilitate safer crossings for pedestrians"

This is the official statement of intent — but it is aspirational language in a bond resolution, not a binding commitment to remove specific street parking.

## 2025 Combined Parking Study — actual data

From the June 29, 2026 work session packet (Progressive Engineering).

**March 12, 2025 (weekday) — peak utilization by category:**
- School parking (363 spots): peaked 80.99% at 8am, 80.44% at 1pm
- Public/city parking (726 spots): peaked 65.70% at 10am, 65.15% at 1pm
- Private/open-to-public (221 spots): peaked ~80% late afternoon

**Key point:** No category hit the 85% benchmark on the study dates. School parking came closest (~81%). The study covered three dates: 3/12/25 (weekday), 5/8/25 (weekday), 5/10/25 (weekend).

## MFCI debt analysis ($9M version)

From the Aug 3, 2026 packet. Prepared by Municipal Financial Consultants, 7/30/2026.

- Principal: $9,000,000
- Total interest: $4,212,282
- Total debt service: $13,212,282
- First-year millage: 0.5922
- Average annual millage: 0.4925
- Assumes 2% annual taxable value growth
- Interest rates as of 7/27/2026 (subject to change)

The city manager prepared three versions ($8M, $9M, $10M). Commission chose $9M.
- $10M version: 0.6570 first year, 0.5471 average
- $8M version: 0.5229 first year, 0.4377 average

## April 2026: Joint Parking Subcommittee recommendation

From the April 20, 2026 commission packet. This is the originating document.

**What was evaluated:**
- Underground parking under the new high school wing: rejected "primarily for safety concerns" (not cost)
- 2, 3, and 4-story deck options on Bagley, shared with city staff fall 2024
- Each option requires 3 feet of city right-of-way

**Subcommittee recommendation:**
- 50/50 city/schools partnership on a three-story deck
- ~213 spaces (grew to 240 by July), estimated $13.6M (grew to $15-18M)
- Operational concept: 1st level public during school day; students/staff on 2nd & 3rd; entire deck public after school, weekends, summer
- "All public parking would be at no charge"

**Design costs:**
- Preliminary design: $190,114 shared equally, 16 weeks (schematic $53,348/6 weeks + design development $138,766/10 weeks)
- To begin May 2026 if approved

**Planned intergovernmental agreement** (to codify):
1. Funding allocations for design and construction
2. Operational expectations
3. Annual maintenance cost funding allocations and projections
4. Other items as needed

**Original financing concept:** $8M issuance, ~$555K annual debt service, 0.4995 mills.

## What the commission chose NOT to do

From the July 20, 2026 packet: the commission had the option of **non-voter-approved LTGO bonds** — borrowing without a public vote, repaid from the general fund. They chose to put it on the ballot instead. (A petition of 10% of registered voters could also have forced a vote on LTGO bonds.)

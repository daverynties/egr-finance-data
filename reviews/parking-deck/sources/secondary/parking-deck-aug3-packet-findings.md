## 1. City Manager memo, July 29 (pp. 2–3)

- **Size:** the design target was "220 +/-" spaces. It reached 240 because "the top level has been extended all the way to the north wall." That level "may be a snow storage area for the winter months," and spaces "can be reduced."
- **Why $10M:** it was "Option 1 in 2028 dollars with roughly $1 million contingency."
- **Debt cost:** "For each $1 million borrowed annual debt service is $73,384."
- **Community feedback:** sessions averaged 30–40 attendees, with "a few comments of support... with mostly opposition expressed, especially those who live in close proximity."
  - Adjacent building owners worry about losing vehicle access through the Bagley lot "for deliveries, on premises parking, and waste hauling."
  - Bagley Condo owners raised "safety, traffic, loading & unloading time, environmental impact."
- **If deferred:** the next ballot chance would be May 2027.

## 2. Bond alternatives memo, July 31 (p. 13)

- **Why lower amounts:** after the July 29 packet went out, "I have been asked could the City set a lower amount than $10 million."
- **Who prepared them:** bond counsel **Roger Swets (Dickinson Wright)** drafted $8M, $9M and $10M versions. "The resolution has been refined as well as the proposed ballot language based on feedback I have received."
- **Amendments:** Swets advised that recitals could be changed at the meeting, but "the ballot language should probably not be altered." So the versions were prepared in advance.
- **Context:** this is the "33-page document" East Insider says arrived 90 minutes before the meeting.

## 5. June 23 presentation (pp. ~64–125): previously unreadable, now read

**Operations as presented to the public:**
- **School day:**
  - "Free public parking in designated spaces"
  - "Permitted and regulated teacher and/or student parking passes in designated parking areas"
- **After the school day:** "Free parking for all on a first come, first serve basis."
- **Non-school days:** "Free parking for all."
- **Compare the Sept 30 school letter:** it promises "no fee" only to school event attendees. Free general-public parking was presented publicly in June but **is not in any binding document**.

**Who it would serve:** students, school visitors and teachers; event parents and visitors (pool, gyms, theater); and "EGR Gaslight Village visitors and workforce."

**Goals:**
- "Increase parking capacity for all school groups and community users"
- "Evaluate the opportunity to add public parking spaces to Gaslight Village"
- "Create over 200 parking spaces"

**Constraints:**
- maintain drop-off and pick-up
- maintain a working truck dock
- match grades at the pool (NE) and PAC (SE) entrances
- an **elevator listed as "optional"**

**Two concepts:**

| Concept | Spaces | Sq ft per space | Drop-off loop |
|---|---|---|---|
| D (chosen) | 240 | 368.8 | No |
| D2 | 229 | 384.7 | Yes |

**Design standards:** 9'×18' stalls, 12' interior drive lanes (city streets are 10'), parking on ramped sections, more than 8' clearance, covered bike and scooter parking, access controls.

**Walk times:** the slide assumes 4 ft/sec for students and Gaslight visitors and 2 ft/sec for elderly users. Examples range from 300' (75 sec) to 2,200' (9 min). The slide's labels for each destination did not extract cleanly.

**Shadow study:** "At its worst case, December 21st, the shadow clears the residences."

The design exercise asked attendees to rank eight look-and-feel images.

## 8. April 2026 materials (pp. ~197–238): one important line

- "**A two-story deck would provide +/-213 parking spaces. This would meet EGRPS's goal of no net loss** of parking capacity in the new design. A three-story deck would create a net gain of 92 spots, and a four-story deck a net gain of 170 spots."
- These are **campus totals**: two-story ≈ 213 (no net loss against 216), three-story (213-space deck) ≈ 308, four-story ≈ 386.

**What the city's money buys (derived):**
- The school's own need is met by a two-story deck (campus about 213–216).
- The 240-space three-level design brings the campus to about 334. The **city-driven increment is about 118 spaces** over the school-only option.
- The city pays half the total cost, about $7.6M–$9.1M, for roughly that increment: **about $64K–$77K per added space for the city**.
- The school pays the other half for the spaces it needs anyway, plus shared site costs.
- No document gives the school-only two-story deck's cost, so the split can't be checked against incremental cost. That remains gap #8.

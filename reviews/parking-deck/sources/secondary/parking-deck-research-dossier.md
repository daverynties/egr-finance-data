## 0. Correction to my first pass

My first version said the deck nets "about 120 spaces, not 240". That compared the wrong baselines. The April 2026 city memo gives the full math: campus 216 today, 144 after the high school project with no deck, and **308 with a 213-space deck**. That puts the surface lot the deck replaces at **about 50 spaces**, roughly the 50-space "Bagley Ave HS" lot in the parking study.

For the 240-space design **(inferred)**:
- **Campus total with the deck:** about 334 spaces
- **Versus the no-deck future (144):** about **+190** spaces
- **Versus before construction (216):** about **+118** spaces
- **Cost per space gained versus the no-deck future:** $15.2M–$18.1M ÷ ~190 ≈ **$80K–$95K**
- **Cost per gross space:** $63K–$75K
- **Critics' "per space for the 72 lost":** about $170K–$250K

## 2. Process facts

- **Subcommittee members:** Commissioners Burdick, Wessely and Schwartz. It met below quorum, so the Open Meetings Act didn't apply and **no meeting content was recorded** ([East Insider](https://eastinsider.substack.com/p/cutting-through-the-noise-on-the)).
- **When it started:** the April city memo and the March 31 school letter say its first meeting was **August 2025**. Muse says May 2025.
- **Aug 3 timing:** the 33-page revised resolution arrived 90 minutes before the meeting, requested by two commissioners (East Insider).
- **No signed agreement:** there is no intergovernmental agreement on cost-sharing yet (FOX 17, Aug 4). The bond only authorizes borrowing. The commission still has to approve bids, the agreement and the bond sale terms (Citizen Portal).
- **School can build alone:** under Michigan law the district only notifies the city and submits plans for state review (FOX 17, July 21). That fits the general rule that the state, not local zoning, governs public school construction in Michigan.
- **District neutrality:** the district must stay neutral under campaign finance law. The vote "will help inform whether the District continues or pauses" the deck ([Sept 30 letter]([Drive link removed])).
- **Design pause:** design is at 50% design development and paused until elected officials give direction ([July 22 deck]([Drive link removed])).
- **Legal costs:** the Sept 8 voucher run shows **$30,477.28 to Varnum LLP for "legal svcs/ballot issues."** It is unclear whether this is the parking bond or the Gaslight litigation ([Sept 8 packet](https://www.eastgrmi.gov/AgendaCenter/ViewFile/Agenda/_09082026-955)).

## 3. Design facts (July 22, 2026 deck: Pure Architects / Fishbeck, "Concept D")

- **Spaces by level:** L0 51, L1 70, L2 67, L3 52, **total 240**. That is 230 standard, 3 compact, 5 accessible and 2 van spaces.
- **Size:** 88,500 sq ft, **368.8 sq ft per space**. Muse's "~300" is the industry rule of thumb, not this design.
- **Ramps and access:** 6.63% and 5.9% ramp slopes, one vehicle entry and one vehicle exit, secure and unsecured bike storage, and a snow-melt area.
- **Security:** cameras, intercoms, "security patrols," glass stair and elevator towers.
- **Five façades:** Option 1 is brick and most expensive; Option 5 is exposed concrete and cheapest.
- **Other costs flagged as extra:** the Bagley/Lake signal, **reconfiguring the high school truck loading dock**, and **snow-melt systems at the Bagley and PAC entrances**. These are to be split 50/50, but the contract mechanism is "to be determined."
- **School-hours split:** about two-thirds of spaces are school-only during school hours (Sept 30 letter). The April concept was L1 public, L2–L3 school.
- **Free parking:** the Sept 30 letter promises no fee only for **school event attendees**. The April city memo said "all public parking would be at no charge" and could offer "free parking in the core of Gaslight Village."
- **Smaller alternative:** a two-story version would hold about 136 spaces (Safe Streets EGR). The Aug 3 minutes mention a "roughly 150-space, two-story deck" in discussion.

## 4. Why the deck is three levels, and the other options (May 27 deck)

- "Initial conceptual school design provided **two story deck** that would have about the same amount of parking being lost."
- "Incremental cost of a **three level deck** provides a unique opportunity to consider adding parking capacity for the entire system."
- **Options shown:** high school concepts A–G (track remains or relocated, all new, etc.), the 2004 concept with surface and underground parking, and an **off-site test fit at 1300 E Beltline** (300 and 780 space scenarios).
- **April city memo options:** 2-, 3- and 4-story decks on Bagley. A **four-story deck = +170 net**. Each option needs 3 ft of city right-of-way.
- **Why underground was dropped:** the April 14 memo says "not feasible primarily for **safety** concerns." The April 24 memo says "limited land available." The City Manager told the commission it was "not feasible." No cost figure for underground appears anywhere.

The estimated interest rate rose from 3.33% to 4.13% between April and July.

- **Ballot wording:** the bonds are **unlimited tax** general obligation bonds, payable within 20 years per series, and can be prepaid.
- **Taxable value assumed:** levy-2026 TV of **$1,096,707,629**, growing 2% a year. The full schedule is in `parking-deck-bond-9M-debt-schedule.csv`.
- **Cost by home value (inferred):** for a home at the census median value of $521,300, taxable value is roughly $200K–$260K depending on purchase year, or about **$100–$130 a year** at the average rate. Year 1 runs about 20% higher.
- **"$5,000 per household" check (inferred):** 3,797 households (Census 2020–24) gives about **$3,480** per household for $13.2M of debt service. About $4,700 if you use the whole $18M project cost. Businesses also pay.
- **Other city money the July 20 memo pointed to:** new state Neighborhood Road Funds (about $320K–$483K a year net) could replace the $1M General Fund transfer to streets and free money for the deck. That source is uncertain because of a legal challenge to the marijuana tax formula ([July 20 packet](https://www.eastgrmi.gov/AgendaCenter/ViewFile/Agenda/_07202026-948)).
- **No-vote route:** the LTGO option needed a 45-day notice of intent, during which petitions from 10% of registered voters could force a vote.

## 12. Gaslight history (critic point 6)

- **Original requirement:** the 2004 Jade Pig / Gaslight PUD said the parking structure "**must be retained and maintained** in order to meet parking requirements" (687 spaces were required). The developer also had to make it available for "**high school sporting events or concerts**."
- **Now:** the 2025 amendment notes the structure "has been removed." The terms for any new structure are deferred to a future PUD agreement ([revised PUD memo](https://eastgrmi.gov/DocumentCenter/View/4370/Revised-PUD-amendment-memo)).
- **Deck history (WalkSafeEGR):** built by Jacobson's in 1991, deferred maintenance in the 2010s, demolition and replacement in 2026. WGVU says the top level was demolished "last year."
- **New concept:** 147 units, 6 buildings including a parking structure, 493 spaces total on 8.6 acres ([city PUD page](https://eastgrmi.gov/594/Gaslight-Investors)). The developer is Gaslight Investors LLC (Scott Wierda, Brian DeVries), which bought the site from bankrupt Jacobson's in 2002 ([Crain's](https://www.crainsgrandrapids.com/news/real-estate/opponents-try-again-to-stop-147-unit-east-grand-rapids-mixed-use-proposal)).
- **Court case:**
  - Approved 4–3 on Oct 6, 2025, over a protest petition.
  - A referendum petition (~1,500 signatures, 1,179 needed) followed, then a lawsuit.
  - **Aug 10, 2026:** Judge George Quist ruled the protest petition valid and the decision legislative. A two-thirds vote was required, so the approval is **void** ([FOX 17](https://www.fox17online.com/news/local-news/east-grand-rapids-ada-cascade/judges-ruling-changes-course-for-east-grand-rapids-development), [WGVU](https://www.wgvunews.org/news/2026-08-11/judge-rules-resident-petition-valid-voiding-approval-for-east-grand-rapids-development)).
  - Sept 21: the City Manager said "there is currently no project in front of us."
  - The Planning Commission had approved Phase 1 (40 units) 7–0 on July 14, 2026.
- **Overlap of people:** many of the same residents (Decker, Anderson, Deems, Baxter, Pattison) are active on both issues.

## 13. The two campaigns

**Safe Streets EGR** ([safestreetsegr.com](https://safestreetsegr.com)): **yes** side.
- Says it was "created independently by an East Grand Rapids resident," with no committee, no paid-for-by line and no names.

**Campaign finance:** ballot question committees for a city question file with the **Kent County Clerk** (campaignfinance@kentcountymi.gov). Kent County does not e-file ballot question committee forms and has no public online search, so the filings (who funds each side) must be requested.

## 15. Gaps filled without FOIA (how each number was derived)

The organizer asked for no FOIA requests, since there isn't time before Nov 3. For each record that isn't published, here is the closest public substitute, with how the figure was built. "Derived" means my arithmetic from the cited inputs.

### Bagley lot space count (what the deck replaces)
- **Answer: about 50 spaces.**
- **Derived:** the April memo gives campus 308 with a 213-space deck and 144 without. 144 − (308 − 213) = 49.
- **Cross-check:** the 2025 parking study lists "Bagley Ave HS" (Area 11) at **50** spaces.
- **Not done:** an aerial count. Imagery downloads are blocked from here; the two published figures agree within one space.

### Annual operating and reserve cost (the city's $160K, rebuilt)

| Component | Basis | Annual |
|---|---|---|
| Operating (lighting, power, cleaning, minor repairs, striping, insurance, routine maintenance) | Walker Consultants' 2026 median **$783/space** × 240 | **~$188,000** |
| Routine + preventive maintenance only | July 22 deck: $150–235/space × 240 | $36,000–$56,000 (already inside the operating line) |
| Capital repair reserve | Walker: at least **1% of construction cost per year** × $15.2M–$18.1M | **~$152,000–$181,000** |
| **Full annual cost** | operating + reserve | **~$340,000–$370,000** |
| **City's half** | 50/50 split (city memo, July 22 deck) | **~$170,000–$185,000/yr** |
| As millage on the 2026 taxable value ($1.097B) | city half ÷ taxable value | **~0.16–0.17 mills** |

- The city's "$160K" matches the operating line alone. It does not appear to include a capital reserve.
- Extras specific to this deck, which push costs toward the high end: heated sidewalks and snow-melt boilers, the elevator, security patrols.
- Walker figures come from its [2026 Saugatuck, Michigan summary](https://www.saugatuckcity.com/uploads/1/3/3/9/133977444/cost_to_build_and_operate_a_parking_structure_in_saugatuck_summary.pdf).

### What a household actually pays
- Michigan taxable value can't exceed state equalized value, which is 50% of market value.
- **Median home** (Census, $521,300): SEV ≈ $260,650, so taxable value is **at most $260,650**.
  - Bond cost: **at most ~$128/yr** on average, ~$154 in year 1, ~$2,570 over 20 years.
  - Homes owned for a long time have lower taxable value because of Proposal A caps, so they pay less.
- **Per household overall:** $13.2M ÷ 3,797 households = **~$3,480** over 20 years. Businesses and non-homestead property also pay, so the residential share is somewhat lower.
- **Per $100K of taxable value:** ~$59 in year 1, $49 on average, ~$41 in the last year (debt schedule).

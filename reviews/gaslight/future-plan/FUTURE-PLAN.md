# Gaslight Village 2040: The Lake Walk Plan
*Joint plan for David (East Grand Rapids, MI), October 2026. Synthesized by Grok Bot from six independent planners and one collaborative round: Grok, Claude (claude-opus-5-5), Codex (gpt-6.1-sol, high), a mobility futurist (Codex), a Dutch/Japanese urbanist (Claude) and a finance/political realist (Codex). This is a concept vision. The 8.6-acre site is privately owned (Gaslight Investors LLCs), so nothing here happens unless the owner, the City and the schools agree. Costs are screening allowances, not estimates.*

## Core idea
**Park once at the edge, then walk from school to village to lake.** One continuous public route, the **Lake Walk**, runs from the high-school crossing on Wealthy diagonally through the Gaslight site to a deeded 1-acre Village Green on Lakeside Dr and on to the Reeds Lake Trail and John Collins Park. Cars arrive at a single wrapped **edge mobility hub** at the west side of the site and stop there. The site interior is car-free (service and emergency vehicles only, on walking-speed woonerf lanes). Wealthy becomes a slow village street, and Lakeside becomes the lake's front porch. All six planners converged on this structure independently.

## 1. Mobility system
- **Pedestrian first.** The Lake Walk is the spine. Raised crossings go at Wealthy/Lakeside, at the high-school entrance and at mid-block. Sidewalks continue unbroken across side streets. Wealthy from Lakeside to about Bagley is posted at 20 mph (citywide limit today: 25), and it stays open to through traffic and buses.
- **Bikes: three loops** (see `maps/05-concept-bike-loops.png`).
  1. *Reeds Lake Ring:* fill the gaps in the existing Reeds Lake Trail, with Lakeside along the site as the showcase; car-free summer Saturdays first.
  2. *School Loop:* Wealthy Elementary → greenways on the grid streets (e.g., Greenwood/Bagley) → Gaslight → high school → Lake Dr, with bell-time school streets.
  3. *Grand Rapids link:* buffered or protected lanes on Lake Dr SE heading west.
  Dutch-scale bike parking at the door: 400+ covered spaces at the high and middle schools, corrals on every Wealthy block face, cargo-bike rooms in every building.
- **Edge mobility hub (the service/parking subsystem).** One shared, wrapped, priced and unbundled garage, entered from Wealthy at the west edge so no car crosses the Lake Walk. It is sized by measurement: study **220–300 spaces** against simultaneous school, Saturday and event peaks (combined surveyed peak 939/1,310 = 72%; school peak 87%). The hub also houses a staffed **bike hall** (300+), a service court for loading, waste and a parcel micro-hub with cargo-bike last 500 ft, accessible drop-off, and shuttle bays. Floors are flat with about 3.5 m floor-to-floor so they can convert. Full underground parking is the premium option only if borings and costs allow.
- **Shuttle → pods.** Phase 1 is a staffed, accessible electric shuttle at peaks (school bells, Saturdays, events). Its loop runs hub → high school → John Collins Park → Lake Dr → Rapid stops, coordinated with The Rapid. Autonomous operation is trialed on the same low-speed route and stops only after it proves winter reliability, accessibility and safe operation around school crossings. Between trips, vehicles wait at the hub, never at the curb.

## 2. Land use
- **About 110–120 homes** at 3 stories on the edges and 4 at the Wealthy core, stepping down toward neighbors. This is lower and smaller than the still-valid 2004 PUD (94 ft). The mix favors downsizers (elevator condos), teachers and young families. Attainable units are recorded with income limits and term.
- **Fine-grain blocks** about 200–260 ft across, with passages every ~150 ft, built by at least three builders and architects.
- **Small storefronts** (400–1,500 sf) along Wealthy and the Lake Walk. Adapt Buildings A/B (77,500 sf) in phases and keep merchants open through construction.
- Off-site growth is handled separately: the 2026 Master Plan's MFR-on-Wealthy idea comes by its own later votes, and the project does not depend on it.

## 3. Public realm and the lake
- **Village Green** (1 acre, deeded to the City) where the Lake Walk meets Lakeside, framing the lake view. Its edges are lined with shopfronts and front doors, never garage walls.
- **Sheltered lane:** arcades and canopies along the Lake Walk, with one glazed signature segment at the Green instead of a full glass roof.
- **Stormwater runnel:** rain gardens treat the first inch before the 36-inch outfall to Reeds Lake, and high-school students monitor and publish water quality. *The plan cleans the lake.*
- **Lake porch:** Lakeside is narrowed along the site, with a raised promenade crossing and an overlook. Summer Saturday open streets come first.
- **February standard:** snow-first clearing of walks and bike routes, south-facing seating, warming shelters, benches every ~50 m, step-free routes.

## 4. Phasing
| When | Delivers |
|---|---|
| **Today (2026–27)** | Count parking by place and time. Tactical pilots on Wealthy and Lakeside (paint, planters, temporary raised crossings), bell-time school streets, 300+ bike racks, cargo-bike delivery and bike-share pilot. Treat the Nov 3 deck vote on its own merits, but if it passes, condition the dollars on an edge, wrapped, convertible hub. City–school–owner term sheet. A charrette written to win a 2/3 vote (MCL 125.3403). |
| **2030** | Hub + bike hall open. Phase 1 (~60 homes), the Green and the east half of the Lake Walk with the stormwater runnel. Permanent Wealthy crossings. Staffed peak shuttle. School Loop greenways. |
| **2035** | Full build (~115 homes). Lake Walk complete. Lakeside lake porch permanent if the Saturdays worked. Reeds Lake Ring closed. Supervised autonomous shuttle pilot on the same route. |
| **2040+** | The car-free core is complete. Car-free hours on Wealthy are trialed only if the gates are met. Hub floors convert to homes, storage or a pod depot as demand falls. |

**Phase gates (published in advance):** tier 2 of the garage only if peak occupancy is above 85% for two years; shuttle expansion only at about 15 riders/hour or more; Wealthy car-free trials only once walk/bike share at school bells is 50% or higher (targets: 50% by 2030, 70% by 2035).

## 5. Cost and funding (screening, 2026 $)
| Component | Allowance | Likely sources |
|---|---:|---|
| Private homes and shops | $60–90M | Private equity/debt; Housing TIF for attainable units |
| Edge hub + bike hall | $12–19M | Nov 3 bond (≤$9M, if passed and only for its authorized purpose), school share, private share |
| Lake Walk, Green, runnel | $6–8M | Brownfield/Housing TIF eligible costs, EGLE/stormwater grants, philanthropy |
| Streets and bike loops | $4–6M | MDOT TAP, Safe Routes to School, city capital |
| Operations (Green, snow, shuttle, scorecard) | ~$0.5–1M/yr | **Gaslight Village Partnership**: net parking revenue plus a small assessment |
The private gap (~$14M in David's earlier Gaslight Green) still has to be re-underwritten. TIF does not close it automatically, and the same increment must not be counted twice.

## 6. Politically realistic in EGR
These can win: tactical pilots, school streets, a deeded Green, cleaner lake water, a smaller wrapped garage, heights lower than 2004, and a public scorecard (crashes, walk/bike share, occupancy, runoff, attainable units). These need years of proof first: a permanent Wealthy closure, off-site density, and any new millage beyond the deck.

## 7. Open questions we'd solve together (caveats)
- **Autonomy.** Will supervised shuttles handle Michigan winters and school crossings by 2035? We keep a staffed fallback either way.
- **Underground.** Can the hub go below grade affordably? We'll know after the 20–50 ft borings.
- **Demand.** Will parking and trips actually fall from the ~6,900 daily trips estimated? The gates let the data decide.
- **Owner and law.** Will the owner join? Which approval path applies after the voided 2025 approval and with the 2004 PUD still valid?
- **Money.** Can TIF, grants and the bond close a gap that today does not pencil?
- **Different emphases.** The team leaned different ways on some points. Futurist, Codex and Realist would keep Wealthy fully open longer and go slower on autonomy. Urbanist and Claude push harder on the curbless street and a covered lane. Urbanist would allow one 5-story corner deep inside the site. Others hold at 4. Garage size ranges from 220 to 400 depending on the planner. The gates are how we settle these.

## Team 'yes, and' refinements (one pass each)
Full text is in `synthesis-addenda/`. Highlights:

### claude
**Yes, and…**

- **Make the Lake Walk a legal right before it's a built one.** In the 2027 term sheet, record a public-access easement along the route, plus the Green deed in escrow. The route then survives a sale, refinancing or stalled phasing. Mark it in paint and planters across today's lot by summer 2027, so people walk it years before anything is built.
- **Add a "no-owner" column to the phasing table.** Wealthy and Lakeside pilots, school streets, the Reeds Lake Ring and the bike parking all sit on public right-of-way or school land, and none of them need Gaslight Investors. Showing that this public half can go ahead either way, and that the plan holds whether the Nov 3 vote passes or fails, strengthens our negotiating position and makes us credible to voters.
- **Give the plan one headline test:** *a 10-year-old can walk alone from Wealthy Elementary to the lake on a February afternoon.* Every gate rolls up to this, and anyone in EGR can judge it.
- **Name who keeps score.** The Partnership publishes the scorecard every quarter, with a 2027 baseline counted before any pilots, so the gates can't be adjusted after the fact.
- **Check the hub entrance at dismissal.** Model queuing on Wealthy at the 3pm bell, so drop-off traffic doesn't back up into the new 20 mph street.
- **Guarantee merchants during construction:** wayfinding, short-term loading spots and a published closure calendar.

### urbanist
**Urbanist "yes, and"**

- **Yes to the edge hub, and bikes get the better spot.** Put the bike hall at the Lake Walk corner of the hub, at grade, with its own door. Then add a rule that every building has bike parking within 15 m of its entrance, while car parking stays at the edge. Riding should be the shortest trip on the site, as in Utrecht.
- **Yes to woonerf lanes, and they still meet fire code.** Lay one curbless surface of brick or pavers, keep a 20 ft clear lane for fire trucks, and use trees, benches and planters that trucks can drive over to keep cars at walking pace. Kids can play there; trucks pass only at walking speed.
- **Yes to small storefronts, and sell them to owners.** Split the frontage into lots 5–8 m wide that can be sold as shop condos, with the option of living above or behind the shop, like a Japanese *shōtengai* (covered shopping street). Local merchants should own their units, not just rent them.
- **A frontage code for the Lake Walk.** Put a door at least every 10 m, keep blank walls under 8 m long, make at least 60% of the ground floor glass, and allow up to 2 m of spill-out space for café chairs, plants and displays.
- **Yes to school streets, and move drop-off to the hub.** The walk from the hub to school is 5 minutes, which makes for quieter, safer bells.
- **Add one more number to the scorecard:** the share of 10-year-olds who travel alone. It's the best single test of whether the streets are working.

### codex
- **Yes, and make the Lake Walk a first-phase obligation.** Open a continuous, accessible school-to-lake route by 2030, using temporary segments where necessary. Record public-access easements before construction, with clear maintenance duties and protection against later closure.

- **Yes, and test the hub against usable parking supply.** Separate publicly available spaces from restricted school and private spaces; model overlapping peaks, construction losses and neighborhood spillover. Make expansion contingent on sustained demand after pricing and shared-parking measures.

- **Yes, and give the Partnership a funded operating agreement.** Assign responsibility for snow clearance, elevators, toilets, shuttle service and stormwater maintenance. Establish replacement reserves and a fallback budget if parking revenue declines or grants expire.

- **Yes, and protect merchants and attainable housing concretely.** Include phased relocation space, delivery access and reopening terms for existing businesses; specify the attainable homes’ count, income bands, affordability duration and enforcement.

- **Yes, and broaden the success gates.** School travel share alone should not trigger Wealthy closures: also assess accessible access, emergency response, bus reliability and diverted traffic. Describe lake benefits as measurable reductions in runoff pollution, with baseline monitoring and published results.
### futurist
- **Yes, and make the shuttle useful before making it autonomous.** Coordinate departures with Rapid connections and publish maximum waits, operating hours and fares. Test the loop’s travel time: a shorter direct connection may serve riders better than one circuit through every destination.

- **Yes, and guarantee access from the hub to every front door.** Provide sheltered, step-free boarding, wheelchair space and phone booking alongside any app. Allow booked accessible transport into the core where walking distance is a barrier. Treat these trips as essential access when applying the 15-riders/hour expansion gate.

- **Yes, and make autonomy an operating decision, independent of the redevelopment schedule.** Require a full winter of supervised testing within a defined operating area, with published results for school crossings, snow-obscured markings, boarding and service interruptions. Specify who assists passengers and retrieves a disabled vehicle; remote supervision alone cannot provide physical help.

- **Yes, and design for fewer cars without assuming that outcome.** Track person-trips, transit connections, parking spillover and empty shuttle miles alongside occupancy. Before adding garage capacity, test pricing and shared-parking agreements. Verify daylight, structural loads and exit requirements before promising conversion of garage floors into homes.
### realist
- **Yes, and make the term sheet a delivery agreement:** specify land contributions, public-access easements, construction milestones, merchant relocation support and responsibility for overruns. Secure the Green and continuous Lake Walk access before public money is committed.

- **Yes, and separate the ballot from the redesign:** any approved bond proceeds must follow the actual authorization. Have bond counsel establish which hub changes fit; do not promise that a favorable vote funds this concept.

- **Yes, and replace the blanket “2/3 vote” assumption with a written approval map:** have land-use counsel identify the applicable PUD amendment, rezoning, protest-petition and voting requirements before the charrette.

- **Yes, and underwrite the first phase independently:** show a sources-and-uses budget, lender-supported housing assumptions, a downside case and committed funding for each public improvement. Count grants only when awarded and TIF only after eligibility, capture and timing are established.

- **Yes, and fund upkeep before opening:** assign snow removal, Green maintenance and shuttle obligations through enforceable agreements. Establish who can authorize an assessment; do not assume a partnership can levy one. Publish an operating budget with reserves and a fallback if parking revenue disappoints.

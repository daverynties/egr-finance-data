# Gaslight Village: Park Once, Walk to the Lake

## 1. Core idea
Make Gaslight Village somewhere you park once, or never, and then walk between the lake, the schools and Wealthy Street. The plan offers residents a clear trade: **less height, fewer parking spaces and less traffic than the 2004 PUD that is still valid**, and in return a car-light village core with a real front door onto Reeds Lake. Parking goes to the edges and serves several uses at once. The middle is for people.

## 2. Mobility system
**Pedestrian first**
- Rebuild Wealthy St SE from Lakeside Dr to about Bagley as a **curbless, 15 mph shared street**. Use raised tables at every crossing, put no curb cuts on Wealthy, and keep it open to buses and through traffic. Close it to cars on Saturday mornings and during festivals.
- Build a **"school spine"**: a continuous raised crossing and plaza linking the Gaslight site (north of Wealthy) to the high school and middle school (south of Wealthy). Students crossing to parking and food is the most important foot traffic in the village.

**Bikes and micromobility**
- Build the **Reeds Lake Ring**: close the gaps in the existing Reeds Lake Trail to a continuous 10–12 ft path, with the Gaslight frontage on Lakeside as the main section.
- Build two **neighborhood greenways** on the grid streets (Greenwood and San Jose or Pinecrest) connecting Wealthy Elementary to the village and the high school. Treat them as Safe Routes to School corridors.
- Add buffered bike lanes on Lake Dr SE toward Grand Rapids.
- Put micromobility docks and secure bike parking at the garage, the Village Green, the high school and John Collins Park.

**Service and parking subsystem**
- Build one **shared, wrapped garage of about 400 spaces** on the Gaslight site, entered from the west or north edge of the site. It replaces 687 entitled spaces and serves residents, retail, the school and lake visitors on time-shared schedules.
- Combined peak occupancy is only 72% (939 of 1,310 spaces), so the area needs better-managed parking, not much more of it. Use paid, dynamically priced public parking and keep weekday blocks for students.
- Put loading underground or tucked into the garage edge. Add a small **delivery micro-hub** so cargo e-bikes handle the last 500 ft.

**Autonomous pods and shuttles**
- Build the garage with flat floors and 10–11 ft clear heights so it can become housing or a mobility hub later.
- Reserve curb space now for a low-speed shuttle loop: Gaslight, high school, Collins Park, Lake Dr and the Rapid connections.

## 3. Land use on the Gaslight site
- **About 110–120 homes** in 3–4 story buildings, stepping down toward Lakeside and the neighbors. Include 15–20% attainable units for teachers and empty-nesters so the project qualifies for Housing TIF.
- **Ground-floor retail and dining** fronting Wealthy and the Green. Phase out Buildings A and B rather than demolishing them first.
- A **1-acre Village Green** deeded to the City and placed on the diagonal between Wealthy and the lake, so the lake can be seen from the main street.
- A small **civic room** for school or library programs, ice-cream-scale retail, and rentals that support the lake.

## 4. Public realm and lake connection
- The Green doubles as **stormwater treatment**. Bioretention cells and a visible rain runnel catch the first inch of runoff before it reaches the 36-inch outfall to Reeds Lake. The lake connection is then ecological as well as visual.
- Narrow Lakeside Dr along the site frontage. Use the space for a **lakeside promenade** with a raised crossing to the water and a short boardwalk or overlook.
- Run continuous tree canopy along Wealthy and make winter programming (rink, market, lights) a design requirement.

## 5. Phasing
- **2026–27.** The Nov 3 bond vote decides the garage path:
  - **If it passes:** build the city/school deck on the Gaslight site, inside a site plan that complies with the PUD. Do not build a standalone deck.
  - **If it fails:** run a pricing and shared-use pilot on the existing 1,310 spaces.
  - **Either way:** paint and plant a tactical pilot of the Wealthy shared street, open the school spine crossing, and adopt a resident-drafted form-based overlay through a supermajority-friendly process.
- **2030.** The garage and first residential building are open, the Green is complete and Wealthy is reconstructed. The Ring and greenways are done.
- **2035.** Second building and civic room. Shuttle pilot if the technology and cost hold up. Parking ratios fall as usage data allows.
- **2040+.** Convert garage floors to housing or a mobility hub if demand falls. Car-free Wealthy hours become routine.

## 6. Rough cost and funding
| Item | Rough cost | Funding |
|---|---|---|
| Shared garage (~400 spaces) | $13–16M | $9M bond if it passes, plus developer share and parking revenue |
| Wealthy shared street | $7–10M | MDOT TAP and Safe Routes grants, city capital |
| Green and stormwater | $3–5M | EGLE and stormwater grants, Housing TIF |
| Ring, greenways, Lake Dr | $3–5M | Trail grants, philanthropy |
| Shuttle pilot | ~$1M/yr | Grant-funded only |

Brownfield TIF applies only where eligibility is real. The roughly $14M gap from the earlier Gaslight Green concept shrinks if the bond money goes into one shared garage instead of two.

## 7. What's politically realistic in EGR
- Any rezoning faces a protest petition and the 2/3 vote it triggers (MCL 125.3403). The winning move is to **stay below the 2004 PUD on every metric**: height, units, parking and trips. Then the plan reads as a reduction, not an upzoning.
- Lead with schools, the lake and safety. Leave density out of the pitch.
- Make height a resident co-design decision, capped at 4 stories.
- Treat the shared street and pricing as reversible pilots, and run them before any permanent changes.
- Don't propose a full car closure of Wealthy. It is the main street and a bus route.

## 8. What's honestly speculative
- **Autonomous shuttles** by 2035 are not certain, so the plan builds for them without depending on them.
- **Trip reductions** below the ~6,900 daily estimate depend on behavior change that hasn't been shown here yet.
- **Retail viability** on a quieter, car-light street is unproven.
- **Funding**: whether the bond passes, and whether brownfield or Housing TIF eligibility holds up, are both open.
- **Developer appetite** for a smaller, cheaper-to-park project depends on interest rates and land cost.
- **Narrowing Lakeside Dr** needs traffic analysis first.

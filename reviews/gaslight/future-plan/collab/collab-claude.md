## Combined direction: "The Lake Walk Compact"

**Ideas to adopt:**
- **Spine:** Grok's diagonal **Lake Walk**, with Urbanist's shotengai character and small storefronts of 400–1,500 sf. It runs from the Wealthy/high-school crossing to a **1-acre Green deeded to the City** at Lakeside.
- **Garage:** the **edge hub** from Grok and Urbanist: wrapped, shared, priced, flat-floored and convertible, with no car crossing the Walk. Size it the way Realist and Codex propose, by studying 220–325 spaces against measured school and Saturday peaks.
- **Process:** everyone agrees on pilots first. Use the realist and futurist approach of a legal approval path built for a 2/3 vote.
- **Lake story:** the visible stormwater runnel from Urbanist and Grok, as the plan's single plain-language public promise.

**Strengthening others' concepts:**
- **Make the Lake Lane buildable.** Use canopies and arcades on building fronts instead of a full glass roof over 10–12 m. That keeps most of the winter comfort at a fraction of the cost and avoids fire-code complications. Glaze only one signature segment at the Green.
- **Make "convertible garage" enforceable.** Write floor-to-floor heights (about 3.5 m or more), flat plates and ramp placement into the November deck conditions. Without these, conversion after 2040 is only a slogan.
- **Make the runnel a teaching asset.** Partner with the high school on water-quality monitoring and publish the readings. That turns lake protection into evidence and gives students ownership.

**Gaps nobody covered:**
1. **Who runs the Green.** Create a **Village Green Conservancy** (City, schools, owner and merchants) funded by a small assessment and a share of parking revenue. It would cover snow clearing, programming and the shuttle. Deeding the land without an operating budget only produces an empty lawn.
2. **Winter as a design test.** Set a "February standard": cleared routes, south-facing seating, warming shelters and lighting. Apply it before any feature is approved.
3. **Construction-phase survival.** Use a merchant continuity plan: temporary storefronts, wayfinding, parking validation and phasing that never closes Wealthy frontage and A/B at the same time.
4. **Phase gates with numbers.** Publish triggers in advance. For example, if peak occupancy stays under 80% for two years, the next garage tier is cancelled. If a shuttle averages above a set riders-per-hour threshold, it is expanded. Pilots then turn into decisions instead of arguments.
5. **One public scorecard.** Track crash and near-miss counts, walk/bike share at school bells, parking occupancy, runoff quality and attainable units delivered. Publish it yearly so the 2/3 coalition can see results.
6. **Seniors and accessibility.** Downsizers are the core housing market, so benches every 50 m, step-free routes and accessible drop-off at the Green itself should be required, not just recommended.

**Funding refinement:**
- Keep the bond, TIF and private capital separately accounted for, as Codex and Realist propose.
- Earmark net parking revenue for the Conservancy and the shuttle. That gives operations a dedicated source and keeps them out of the general fund.

**The bargain to voters:** lower and smaller than the 2004 PUD, a deeded Green, cleaner lake water, safer school walks, and published numbers that decide what gets built next.

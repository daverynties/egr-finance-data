## Adopt as the backbone
- **Grok's diagonal Lake Walk**, used as the site's main shopping street rather than a park path. Combine it with **Claude's school spine** so the two form one continuous pedestrian route from the high school crossing to Lakeside.
- **Codex's and the realist's "measure first, then size the garage."** Take the low end (220–300 spaces) and spend the savings on bike parking and public space.
- **Grok's 400–1,500 sf storefronts** and **the futurist's retention of Buildings A/B.** Old buildings and cheap small units are how a local bakery gets started.
- **Claude's tactical pilot on Wealthy.** Paint and planters in 2027 build the case for the 2/3 vote better than renderings.

## Gaps to fill
**Fine-grain blocks.** Divide the 8.6 acres into a grid of blocks about 200–260 ft across, with mid-block passages every 150 ft or so. Sell or lease the parcels to at least three builders and require different architects. That gives Dutch-style variety and keeps the project from reading as one megaproject, which is easier for voters to accept.

**Woonerf interior.** Make the internal service lanes true woonerven: one surface, no curbs, walking speed, benches and planters placed to slow cars. Deliveries and emergency access can still get through, and children can play there.

**Bike parking at Dutch scale.** Grok's hub bike hall is a good start, but parking has to be at the door to get used:
- 400 or more covered spaces at the high and middle schools. That is fundable through Safe Routes to School and is the cheapest way to cut drop-off traffic.
- Bike corrals of 8–12 spaces in place of one car space on every block face along Wealthy.
- Secure, ground-floor bike rooms in every residential building, sized for cargo bikes.

**Shotengai-style street management.** Form a Gaslight merchants' association, funded by a small business district assessment. It would handle:
- shared awnings or a continuous canopy along Wealthy for winter
- unified lighting and banners
- seasonal events and keeping storefronts lively

Japanese shopping streets survive because merchants run them together. That is also how to answer the "retail viability is unproven" concern raised in Claude's plan.

**Winter-first maintenance.** Clear snow from bike routes and the Lake Walk before car lanes, as Oulu, Finland does. This costs almost nothing, is very visible, and keeps winter riding year-round.

**Children's independent mobility as the measure of success.** Track the share of Wealthy Elementary and high school students who walk or bike on their own. Set targets of 50% by 2030 and 70% by 2035. Parents understand this measure, and it gives the plan a goal that isn't about cars.

## Strengthening others' ideas
- **Lakeside "lake porch"** (Grok, Claude): start with car-free summer Saturdays as a weekly street festival with bikes welcome. Make it permanent only once it has fans.
- **Shuttle** (everyone): before any pods, put cargo-bike delivery from the micro-hub and a bike-share fleet with child seats in the 2027 pilot. Both are cheaper and fit the village scale better.
- **Village Green** (everyone): line its edges with ground-floor shops and front doors, never blank garage walls. Good neighbors at the edges make a green feel safe. Its size matters less.

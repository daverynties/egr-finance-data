## Combine around one promise: school to village to lake

Adopt **Claude’s “park once” framework and school spine**, **Grok’s diagonal Lake Walk**, and **Urbanist’s small storefronts and sheltered lane** as one organizing plan: a continuous, accessible route from the school crossing through shops and a public Green to Lakeside and existing waterfront destinations. Use **Futurist’s year-round accessibility** and **Realist’s enforceable public benefits** to make that promise dependable.

**Make the Lake Walk the first deliverable.** Secure its permanent public easement with redevelopment approval. Build the school crossing, an initial planted gathering space and the Lakeside crossing alongside the first occupied building. Add arcades and short covered sections at shop entrances and gathering points; these give Urbanist’s winter shopping concept a practical first phase. Include benches with backs, public restrooms, drinking water, lighting and a funded snow-clearing route.

**Turn the shared garage into a district agreement.** Combine everyone’s edge-parking concept with Realist’s location-specific demand analysis. City, school and owner should agree on school-hour allocations, resident access, evening sharing, accessible spaces, pricing and overflow before selecting capacity. Commission one concept design and operating budget, with expansion options where feasible. Evaluate any borrowing against its authorized purpose and document each party’s contribution.

**Give local businesses a place in the financing plan.** Pair Urbanist’s small shops with Claude’s staged adaptation of Buildings A/B. Offer existing merchants phased relocation, clear construction access and advance notice. Create several modest, move-in-ready spaces for local businesses, with shared storage and waste handling. Use temporary markets to identify tenants before committing permanent retail space.

**Make housing and public space commitments durable.** Combine Futurist’s subsidy-backed attainable housing with Realist’s recorded commitments: specify affordability duration, eligible households and delivery milestones. Establish Green ownership, drainage maintenance and annual programming funding before construction. A city–school–owner implementation group should publish a single budget covering capital costs, operations and contingency.

**Let pilots unlock the next investment.** Track school-crossing comfort, parking occupancy by location, merchant sales trends, accessible-route usability and stormwater performance. Publish results before permanent street reconstruction or shuttle expansion.

The lovable centerpiece is an everyday village: students meeting after school, seniors walking under cover, families pausing on the Green, and the lake visible ahead.

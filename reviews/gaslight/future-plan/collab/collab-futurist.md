**Combine the Lake Walk, school spine and edge hub into one mobility system.** Adopt Grok’s diagonal Lake Walk, Claude’s school crossings and Reeds Lake Ring, and Urbanist’s covered lane and bike hall. Together they make the hub an inviting arrival point: park or step off transit, shelter from snow, then reach shops, schools and the lake along a clear, accessible route.

- **Make transit useful before adding autonomy.** Build on Codex’s human-driven circulator and Realist’s Rapid coordination. First identify which existing connections need better frequency, shelters or timed transfers. Pilot a staffed electric shuttle only where it closes a measured gap, with a simple published timetable and boarding available without a smartphone. Align service with school activities, shopping and events; include evening return trips.

- **Give the village a winter mobility promise.** Extend Urbanist’s covered-lane idea into maintained routes between boarding points, accessible parking, the Green and storefronts. Fund snow clearance, drainage, lighting and sheltered seating alongside construction. Provide level boarding, wheelchair space, stroller access and understandable wayfinding. These features also become the foundation for future autonomous service.

- **Treat the curb as shared infrastructure.** Strengthen Claude’s delivery micro-hub and Realist’s service edge with clearly assigned spaces for accessible pickup, shuttle boarding, deliveries and ride-hailing. Separate boarding from bicycle movement. Manage delivery windows around school peaks, and require future autonomous vehicles to wait at the edge hub between trips.

- **Design flexibility into the hub’s construction contract.** Adopt the shared, wrapped garage proposed across the plans. Commission an adaptation study before fixing its structure: test daylight, floor loads, utilities and ramp removal for specific future uses. Install expandable electrical capacity and standard charging equipment. Reserve space for bicycle repair, parcel lockers and mobility assistance from opening day.

- **Give operations an owner and a budget.** Combine Realist’s maintenance agreements with Grok’s operating partnership. Establish a city–school–merchant–Rapid working group, with one accountable service operator. Budget a two-year pilot separately from capital works, using committed partner contributions and conservatively estimated parking revenue.

**Let autonomy earn expansion.** Start with a supervised pilot on a defined route only after demonstrating accessible boarding, winter reliability and safe school crossings. Expand when cost per passenger, waiting times and completed trips compare favorably with staffed service. The same welcoming stops and routes should serve residents through every technological stage.

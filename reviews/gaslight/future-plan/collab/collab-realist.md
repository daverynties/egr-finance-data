Adopt **Claude’s “park once, walk to the lake,” Grok’s diagonal Lake Walk, Urbanist’s small storefronts and sheltered passages, Codex’s demand-based parking, and Futurist’s joint city–school–owner process**. Together they offer EGR a concrete bargain: safer school access, a permanent green, useful shops and homes that let residents stay in the community.

- **Make one negotiated agreement the backbone.** Before final design, establish a city–school–owner term sheet covering land control, garage ownership, shared-use hours, construction sequencing, public access, maintenance and financial responsibility. Translate those commitments into recorded easements and enforceable development agreements. Give neighboring residents and merchants a defined role in shaping height transitions, delivery hours and construction access.

- **Combine the parking ideas through evidence and contracts.** Study the proposed 250–400-space range against simultaneous demand, then select capacity. Reserve school access through an explicit agreement while keeping evening and weekend spaces available to village visitors. Price convenient short stays differently from all-day parking, retain accessible drop-offs, and offer merchant validation. Allocate costs according to documented use and benefit.

- **Build a finance package with distinct accounts.** Private capital funds revenue-producing buildings; public contributions purchase specified public benefits. Evaluate Michigan brownfield and Housing TIF eligibility through an eligible-cost schedule and projected increment. Model capture duration, reimbursement timing and effects on affected taxing jurisdictions. Assign any voter-approved borrowing only to uses permitted by the ballot and applicable authority. Secure grant awards, local matches and bridge funding before treating them as construction money.

- **Make attainable housing a deliverable.** Pair any housing subsidy with explicit income limits, rents, affordability duration, monitoring and repayment provisions. Include smaller accessible homes and family-sized units. Explain the community benefit through teachers, workers and residents seeking to downsize, with eligibility rules established in the agreement.

- **Make the public realm affordable to keep lovable.** Start Urbanist’s sheltered lane with selective arcades and wind-protected seating. Fund snow clearance, tree care, stormwater maintenance and programming in an annual operating budget. Support small shops through phased construction, clear signage and practical leasing terms.

Authorize construction when financing closes, public access is secured and the approval pathway is established. Deliver the green, principal school crossing and lake connection with the first occupied phase, giving residents visible benefits from the outset.

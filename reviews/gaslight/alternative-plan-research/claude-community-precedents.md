## 1. What residents and officials want or oppose

### Residents: recurring objections
- **Height and scale.** This is the main objection. Comment cards from the Feb 17, 2025 hearing ask for "3–4 stories is plenty" and "Limit the height to 4 stories" (MAY5:5384, 5440–5442, 5455, 5471). Letters say "Three stories is plenty, some green space" (MAY5:7293) and ask to cap buildings at 4 stories "to maintain the village's charm" (MAY5:6527–6529). On Facebook the plan "looks like a college campus" (FB:12).
- **Too little green or public space.** Comments include "Almost all pavement and no green space" (FB:46), "a strip mall that caters to cars, not pedestrians" (MAY5:10208), and "minimal green space" (FAQ:6909). One resident sent a sketch comparing a green to "Central Park" (MAY5:8053–8055). Since the Aug 2026 ruling, Facebook posts have floated a "Village Green," a farmers market, or a city purchase of the lots (FB:16, 56).
- **Traffic and safety near schools.** Concerns cover drop-off backups and children walking to school (MAY5:5460–5463, 5507–5512). One commenter said they would not want their children's school next to Breton Village (FAQ:6937–6938). Residents also object to the lack of a full traffic or safety study (FAQ:7169; FB:15).
- **Parking.** Residents doubt that 1 stall per unit is enough (MAY5:5501, 5531). They resent the demolished deck because the developer had "committed to providing and maintaining a parking structure" (FB:50; FAQ:7177–7179).
- **Affordability skepticism.** Residents ask the city to "define 'affordable'" (MAY5:5532). One letter calls the "affordable" housing "a joke" (MAY5:6773). Another asks to raise the share to 20–25% (MAY5:6534). A resident who once lived in subsidized housing doubts that people in need could afford these units (FAQ:7114–7115).
- **Tax incentives.** Residents object to Brownfield/TIF reimbursement, though the "$20M+" figure circulating online has no source (FB:14, 54). One comment says the project would contribute "zero tax dollars" (MAY5:5477–5478).
- **Process and trust.** An opposition group says 85% of public input is opposed. It also says staff skewed comparisons by measuring heights against the hospital and the high-school tower (FAQ:7147, 7159–7160). Other complaints are private streets (FAQ:7171; MAY5:6536) and one owner controlling much of the village (FAQ:6904–6906).
- **Other.** Comments raise retail vacancy and high rents (FAQ:6899–6903; MAY5:5425, 5499), tree loss (MAY5:5522), flat roofs (MAY5:5402), and requests for 3D mockups that show surrounding buildings (FAQ:7100–7105).

### Residents: what they asked for or supported
- **Pedestrians first.** One letter asks for parking to go to the garage, keeping only accessible stalls near storefronts. It also asks for open space between buildings, shaded seating and natural play features (FAQ:6859–6867).
- **Senior-friendly, walkable housing.** One comment calls this a "marketing opportunity" (MAY5:5533–5534).
- **Supporters' points.** The site has been an eyesore for about 20 years. Supporters also cite property rights, the tax base, housing diversity, and the fact that the plan already shrank from 7 stories to 5 (FB:18–23).
- **June 2024 open house themes.** Excitement about new development, more green and public space, concern about a 7-story building, parking concerns, no roundabout, and the question of what added housing means for schools (FAQ:761–778).

### Officials' stated concerns
- **Commissioners in June 2025.** Hunter spoke on "density and to make sure the character of East is upheld." Groff-Blaszak asked for an open work session on the standards. Wessely and Schwartz framed the vote as a legal standards test (FAQ:5364–5377).
- **Alternative layouts from commissioners.** Skaggs presented a drawing that included open public space (FAQ:7046). Some commissioners suggested a pedestrian mall on the southern private road. Staff cautioned that almost 90% of pedestrian malls failed, and suggested temporary event closures and complete-street design instead (FAQ:1451–1462).
- **Staff on the open space.** Staff said the "resident amenity area" behind Building E "may not satisfy the interest in having a larger public gathering space." They suggested swapping Building E with the open space. This became Condition 8: the open space should be "a more prominent site feature" (FAQ:848–851, 1274–1288).
- **Other conditions.** Upper floors above 3 stories must step back (FAQ:807). The City is not obligated to fund infrastructure (FAQ:837–843).
- **Master Plan adoption.** When adopting the plan, the Commission removed "the Gaslight Investors example" (Minutes__03162026:107–114).
- **Sept 2026.** Schwartz reported hearing from residents who accept "the compromises," and Hunter noted "an uptick in people that are in support." Groff-Blaszak said "there is a bias on who shows up" (AG-09212026:1996–2010). The Mayor said "the project is not moving forward," and Skaggs said "this version is not moving forward" (AG-10052026:1466–1471).

## 2. 2026 Master Plan goals for Gaslight Village

- **Vision:** "ongoing investment and enhancements in the Gaslight Village business district, a variety of housing options… ample preserved open spaces" (MP:489–494).
- **Goals (MP:506–516):**
  - Housing "to accommodate aging-in-place, new families, and young professionals at an attainable cost"
  - "Provide open spaces in Gaslight Village for social interaction and community events"
  - "screening parking lots… promoting a walkable environment"
- **Recommendations (MP:1055, 1063–1068):**
  - #2 increase housing variety and attainability
  - #6 create or enhance public spaces in Gaslight Village and Collins Park and "highlight Reeds Lake"
  - #7 infill on the Plaza along Wealthy
  - #8 a western gateway
- **Plaza concept sketches.** Every sketch keeps the grocery and parking and adds "creation of prominent open space," connectivity to the PUD site, and a public plaza. The sketches depend on the owners (MP:684–693, 699–739).
- **Survey results.** Respondents call the district "tired." Their most-wanted businesses are a sit-down restaurant, coffee shop, pharmacy, hardware store and clothing store (MP:296–304). 26.5% would pick a non-single-family home next. About 44% would leave EGR, citing taxes and children finishing school (MP:328–332).
- **Parking.** The May 2025 study was "consistent with the 2018 study," which found parking sufficient. Parking still needs restudy after the high-school expansion and the PUD (MP:602–611).
- **Out of date.** The plan still calls the concept "approved… in 2025" (MP:624–625).

The precedents point to three lessons [inference]:
- Strong public green space was usually funded or owned by the public: Northville's city-owned river park, Edina's TIF-funded plaza, Wilmette's grant-funded green.
- Parking that is replaced and shared is a common bargaining chip.
- Large approvals can still stall when market conditions change, as at Okemos.

## 4. Design ideas that address the objections

- **Stepped heights.** The city already requires upper floors above 3 stories to step back (FAQ:807). Staff note the taller buildings are clustered at the south near the commercial core, with only 41 of 149 units on the 515 Lakeside parcel (FAQ:1265, 1290–1293). The adjacent Lakewood Hills consent agreement allows up to 58 ft (FAQ:1244–1247). Wilmette used 5-ft setbacks on the upper floors. One option is 3 stories at the edges and 4 only at the core [inference], which would match the many "3–4 stories" comments.
- **A central green or Village Green.** Staff's swap of Building E and the open space (FAQ:1278–1279), the Master Plan's "prominent open space" (MP:690), and the event-flexible street that staff preferred over a pedestrian mall (FAQ:1460–1462) all point the same way. A green could line up with a new Plaza courtyard and the Reeds Lake axis (MP:1063–1065) [inference].
  - **Ownership models:** dedication to the city (Northville), a public easement, or a TIF-funded plaza (Edina). Each needs a maintenance agreement [inference].
- **Hidden or shared parking.** Options include lots behind buildings (Ada), parking below grade or wrapped by buildings (Edina), and garage-first circulation (FAQ:6859–6862).
  - **Existing parking data:** Gaslight lots were only 59% used on a typical weekday in 2018 (FAQ:1370–1371). In June 2026 no part of the system exceeded 85% (PD).
  - **The parking-deck bond:** a separate Nov 3, 2026 bond would build a 240-space public deck on Bagley at about $60k/space (PD). A shared-parking plan that relies on that deck, or replaces it, would directly affect the bond debate [inference].
- **Design-detail asks:** pitched roofs, tree preservation, 3D context mockups, kid-friendly play features, and no private-street lockout (MAY5:5402, 5522; FAQ:6866–6868, 7100–7105, 7171).

## 5. Attainable-housing tools that work in Michigan

- **Mandatory inclusionary zoning is effectively off the table.** MCL 123.411 bars local rent control. Since 2019 it allows "voluntary incentives and agreements" to increase moderate- and low-cost rentals ([MCL 123.411](https://ombudsman.legislature.mi.gov/documents/mcl/pdf/MCL-123-411.pdf)). Density bonuses in a PUD or development agreement are therefore the lever [inference; no case law found]. Wilmette-style payments in lieu of units are an Illinois practice; whether they are legal in Michigan is unverified.
- **Housing TIF (PA 90 of 2023, Brownfield Act).** It covers income-qualified housing up to 120% AMI and allows mixed-income projects. Capturing school taxes requires MSHDA review ([MSHDA](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/about/press-releases/2023/10/02/mshda-board-approves-housing-tif-program-to-create-and-preserve-affordable-housing); [Grand Rapids guidelines](https://www.grandrapidsmi.gov/media/1qxnld4q/bra-housing-tif-interim-guidelines-overview-2-pager-final_2.pdf)).
  - **Kent County BRA:** established Feb 2024; 15-year capture cap, extendable for housing ([KCBRA policy](https://www.kentcountymi.gov/DocumentCenter/View/6975/KCBRA-Policy-for-Administration-of-Brownfield-Redevelopment-and-Housing-Projects-PDF)).
  - **First KCBRA plan:** in Lowell, 17 units with 5 at ≤120% AMI and a $750k plan, $700k of which fills the gap for those units ([Crain's GR](https://www.crainsgrandrapids.com/news/real-estate/kent-county-taps-new-housing-incentives-for-first-time-to-support-lowell-project)).
  - **Other local examples:** Kentwood has 10 of 216 homes at ≤120% AMI for 10 years; Grand Rapids' 443 Sheldon has 20 of 26 ([Kent County search results](https://www.grandrapidsmi.gov/media/p1vhe4pc/443-sheldon-ave-se-brownfield-public-hearing-09-23-2025.pdf)).
  - **EGR has already been briefed:** an Oct 21, 2024 Fishbeck presentation to EGR on using Brownfield TIF for workforce housing ([city document](https://eastgrmi.gov/DocumentCenter/View/4134/Oct-21-2024-Fishbeck-presentation-An-Old-Tool-with-a-New-Use-Using-Brownfield-TIF-for-Workforce-Housing); text not extracted).
- **PILOT (MSHDA Act §15a, amended by PA 239 of 2022).** Cities may grant a service charge in lieu of taxes for housing up to 120% AMI, typically with 15-year affordability. Grand Rapids and Detroit adopted workforce PILOT ordinances in 2024 ([Grand Rapids](https://www.grandrapidsmi.gov/departments/community-development/payment-in-lieu-of-taxes-pilot/workforce-housing-pilot-for-rental-projects/); [Detroit template](https://detroitmi.gov/sites/detroitmi.localhost/files/2025-08/PILOT%20affordability%20agreement%20template%20final.pdf)). EGR would need its own ordinance [inference].
- **Attainable Housing Facilities Act (PA 236 of 2022).** It gives up to 12 years of exemption, but only for rental buildings of 4 or fewer units. That might fit townhome clusters [inference] ([MML](https://mml.org/inside208/?p=8968)).
- **MSHDA Missing Middle program.** The ARPA money is gone and applications are closed ([MSHDA](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/developers/missing-middle); [Crain's](https://prod.crainsdetroit.com/node/934491)).
- **Why 100–120% AMI is criticized.** EGR's median household income is $168,487, against Kent County's $79,756 (MP:156). Units at 100–120% of county AMI may rent near or above EGR's $1,845 median rent (MP:158) [inference]. LIHTC generally serves ≤80% AMI, so it would not cover these units [inference, not verified here].

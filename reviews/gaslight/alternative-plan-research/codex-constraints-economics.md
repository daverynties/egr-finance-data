# Gaslight Investors site: research brief

*Research reviewed through October 9, 2026. Interpretations and calculations are marked [inference].*

## 1. Current zoning, the 2004 PUD, and the court ruling

**The property is zoned PUD; ordinary C-1 and multifamily standards are comparison benchmarks, not its governing development entitlement.** City staff states that the original 2004 Jade Pig PUD remains valid, while the unbuilt 2008 amendment expired. Construction still requires an appropriate final plan. ([July 2025 city memo, lines 685–707](local:/workspace/gaslight/sources/FAQs-Document.txt:685); [original approval provisions reproduced in amendment memo, §1](local:/workspace/gaslight/sources/Revised-PUD-amendment-memo.txt:1294))

The original approval authorized **up to 107 condominiums**, commercial uses limited by Exhibit E, and **687 parking spaces**, reduced from ordinary ordinance calculations of approximately 750–812. Its tallest building could reach **seven stories, 79 feet to the roof midpoint and 94 feet to the peak**. Wealthy Street setback relief allowed development at or near the front property line; the reviewed text does not establish a complete numerical setback schedule for every original building. ([PUD findings, paragraphs m–n](local:/workspace/gaslight/sources/Revised-PUD-amendment-memo.txt:2416); [height comparison](local:/workspace/gaslight/sources/FAQs-Document.txt:1930))

The original conditions required retaining and maintaining the parking structure, city approval of parking restrictions, and availability for school sporting events or concerts consistent with property owners’ and tenants’ needs. Enlarging Exhibit E uses required city approval supported by adequate parking. ([PUD §3(j)](local:/workspace/gaslight/sources/Revised-PUD-amendment-memo.txt:1434); [§3(o)](local:/workspace/gaslight/sources/Revised-PUD-amendment-memo.txt:1493))

The city’s 2025 comparison identifies these ordinary district standards:

| Standard | C-1 comparison: Wealthy parcel | MFR comparison: Lakeside parcel |
|---|---|---|
| Height | 40 feet; three stories | 35 feet; 2½ stories |
| Setbacks | Front, side, rear: zero | Front: 10–25 feet; side: 10; street side: 12; rear: 25 |
| Residential density | No fixed numerical density ceiling identified in city FAQ | Minimum 2,100 square feet of land/unit |
| Residential parking | 1.5 spaces/unit | 1.1–1.8 spaces/unit, depending on bedrooms |
| Commercial parking | Depends on use | Depends on applicable use |

Sources: [May 5 packet, Table 3](local:/workspace/gaslight/sources/May5-2025-CC-packet-with-studies.txt:263); [city density FAQ](local:/workspace/gaslight/sources/FAQs-Document.txt:1869). **[inference]** The 107-unit PUD authorization, rather than a theoretical whole-site MFR calculation, is the relevant existing residential limit.

On **August 10, 2026**, Judge George Quist ruled the residents’ protest petition valid; the city’s October 6, 2025 **4–3 approval was therefore insufficient and voided**. ([WGVU report](https://www.wgvunews.org/news/2026-08-11/judge-rules-resident-petition-valid-voiding-approval-for-east-grand-rapids-development?_amp=true); [city project page](https://www.eastgrmi.gov/594/Gaslight-Investors))

Michigan Zoning Enabling Act **MCL 125.3403** requires a two-thirds legislative-body vote when a qualifying protest precedes final action on a city/village zoning amendment. Signatures must represent owners of at least **20% of affected land**, or **20% of land within 100 feet outward from its boundary**; publicly owned land is excluded. **[inference]** Two-thirds of seven members means five affirmative votes. A future amendment encountering a qualifying protest would face that threshold; the statute does not automatically impose it on every conforming final-site-plan review. ([MZEA §403](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/documents/mcl/pdf/mcl-Act-110-of-2006.pdf))

September 15 minutes record counsel’s position that the ruling invalidated the later vote, leaving the earlier PUD and pending application intact. They describe an interim order and an October 9 hearing; this research did not verify that hearing’s outcome. ([Minutes reproduced in October 13 packet, lines 148–169](local:/workspace/gaslight/sources/agendas/Agenda__10132026-961.txt:148))

## 2. Parcels, buildings, infrastructure, and physical conditions

The approximately **8.6-acre** site comprises **41-14-33-276-032**, 2255 Wealthy Street, and **41-14-33-276-029**, 515 Lakeside Drive. The April 2024 application identifies owners **2255 Wealthy, LLC** and **515 Lakeside, LLC**, with **Gaslight Investors, LLC as sole member** and Scott Wierda as contact; this establishes application ownership, not a current title search. ([City parcel memo](local:/workspace/gaslight/sources/FAQs-Document.txt:690); [application, ownership section](https://www.eastgrmi.gov/DocumentCenter/View/4479/Feedback-from-Cascade-Township-Building-Inspections-Services); [site area](local:/workspace/gaslight/sources/May5-2025-CC-packet-with-studies.txt:2240))

Existing **Buildings A and B are two stories**, totaling **77,500 usable square feet of C-1 uses** in the August 2025 drawing. That revision proposed 32,000 additional commercial square feet, 147 dwellings and 493 parking spaces; these describe the invalidated proposal, not an approved replacement entitlement. ([August 27 concept, use/parking summary](local:/workspace/gaslight/sources/Aug-27-2025-Revised-Gaslight-Investors-Concept-Plan.txt:105))

The former deck’s upper level was demolished in early 2025. City records describe an agreement allowing temporary lower-level parking after demolition of the aging structure. **[inference]** Original garage obligations and subsequent demolition/access agreements must be read together before assuming either permanent public access or release from parking obligations. ([City history FAQ](local:/workspace/gaslight/sources/FAQs-Document.txt:1835); [January 21, 2025 minutes](https://eastgrmi.gov/Archive/ViewFile/Item/1564))

Utility reviews were favorable **preliminary capacity opinions**: sanitary service includes existing A/B collectors; Lakeside water mains were replaced in 2014, and Wealthy mains lined in 2016. Taller buildings may need private pressure boosters. Stormwater reaches Reeds Lake through a 36-inch system and an existing separator near John Collins Park. Final utility modeling and drainage design remained necessary. ([May packet, utility review](local:/workspace/gaslight/sources/May5-2025-CC-packet-with-studies.txt:3701))

Prein & Newhof’s environmental opinion considered contamination unlikely from the summarized medical, retail and residential history. Its geotechnical opinion anticipated conventional foundations but requested **20–50-foot borings** and acknowledged possible deep foundations. These are opinions, not verified Phase I/II environmental assessments or boring results. **[inference]** Neither contamination status nor foundation cost is settled by these letters. ([Environmental review](local:/workspace/gaslight/sources/May5-2025-CC-packet-with-studies.txt:3596); [soils review](local:/workspace/gaslight/sources/May5-2025-CC-packet-with-studies.txt:3644))

## 3. Traffic, shared parking, and the public-deck ballot

Fleis & VandenBrink’s April 18, 2025 analysis estimated **6,945 daily vehicle trips after modal adjustments**, versus 6,738 for the fully developed 2004 PUD. School dismissal estimates were **514 versus 539 trips/hour**, but Saturday peaks were **672 versus 384**. **[inference]** “Similar traffic” is period-specific; comparison with an unbuilt entitlement does not measure the increase over today’s partly vacant site. ([May packet, Table 6](local:/workspace/gaslight/sources/May5-2025-CC-packet-with-studies.txt:3202))

Its shared-parking model estimated **395 spaces on weekday evenings and 397 at weekend noon**, against **523 proposed spaces**, including 54 reserved spaces in demand. Assumptions included a 5% retail modal reduction and 20% resident-to-retail captive share. **[inference]** Subtraction produces a 126–128-space cushion; the table’s printed surplus is two spaces too high. ([Tables 7 and assumptions](local:/workspace/gaslight/sources/May5-2025-CC-packet-with-studies.txt:3218))

Progressive AE accepted the general methodology but requested current school-session counts, intersection operations, five-year crash data, pedestrian/bicycle assessment and further parking verification. It could not independently verify the 523-space supply. The August revision reduced supply to **493**, explicitly deferring the final shared-parking study. **[inference]** The April modeled cushion cannot simply be carried into that revision. ([Consultant review](local:/workspace/gaslight/sources/May5-2025-CC-packet-with-studies.txt:3409); [review requirements](local:/workspace/gaslight/sources/May5-2025-CC-packet-with-studies.txt:3505); [August parking note](local:/workspace/gaslight/sources/Aug-27-2025-Revised-Gaslight-Investors-Concept-Plan.txt:119))

For the separate public/school deck, the November 3, 2026 ballot concerns **up to $9 million in city borrowing**. The corrected resolution estimates average additional millage of **0.4925 mills**; the borrowing ceiling does not establish the complete joint project budget. ([August council minutes/resolution](local:/workspace/gaslight/sources/agendas/Agenda__08172026-953.txt:866); [millage correction](local:/workspace/gaslight/sources/agendas/Agenda__08172026-953.txt:926))

The school parking memo anticipated reducing the senior lot from **111 to 40 spaces**, displacing 71 vehicles. It identified substantial nearby vacancies, recommended testing parking/busing alternatives first, and warned that a Bagley garage could concentrate arrival and dismissal queues. Its conceptual deck options ranged from **136 to 291 spaces**. ([November 10 traffic memo, PDF pp. 2–4](../../parking-deck/sources/primary/parking-deck-traffic-memo-2025-11-10.pdf))

**[inference]** Calculations from three surveyed dates show maximum combined occupancy of **939/1,310 spaces, or 71.7%**. Separate public and school peaks were 65.7% and 86.8%; district-wide vacancies therefore do not establish convenient availability at each destination or during simultaneous events. ([Utilization CSV, date/hour/category rows](../../parking-deck/sources/primary/parking-deck-utilization-2025.csv))

**[inference]** School displacement, Gaslight customer parking, private PUD requirements and construction-period losses require one coordinated demand/access analysis. Public spaces cannot automatically satisfy private requirements, and shared spaces cannot be counted twice during overlapping demand. ([Original school-event condition](local:/workspace/gaslight/sources/Revised-PUD-amendment-memo.txt:1434); [school memo, pp. 2–4](../../parking-deck/sources/primary/parking-deck-traffic-memo-2025-11-10.pdf))

Current small Gaslight storefront asking rents are **$38.50–45/square foot/year, triple net**, excluding additional property expenses and utilities. ([2182–2184 Wealthy listing](https://www.loopnet.com/Listing/2182-2184-Wealthy-St-SE-Grand-Rapids-MI/42014072/))

City assessment records show selected village condominium sales of **$622,000–929,000 during 2023–2024**. **[inference]** Dividing full sale prices by recorded floor areas gives approximately **$303–530/square foot**; the assessment table’s own square-foot column instead uses building residual value. ([2026 ECF study, Neighborhood 15, PDF pp. 62–65](https://www.eastgrmi.gov/DocumentCenter/View/3027/2026-Residential-ECF-Study?bidId=))

**[inference]** A project pencils when supported rental income or net condominium proceeds cover construction, land, parking, fees, financing, contingency and required returns. Illustratively—not a market forecast—$2,500 monthly rent, 95% collection, 35% operating expenses and a 6% capitalization rate imply **$308,750 value/unit**. Shared corridors increase gross construction area beyond rentable area; parking can materially consume the margin. These are stated hypothetical assumptions, not Gaslight underwriting.

### Act 381 and housing TIF

Michigan **Act 381** permits approved capture of increased property taxes to reimburse eligible redevelopment costs. EGLE handles environmental activities; MEDC/MSF handles eligible nonenvironmental activities. Housing TIF added through **Public Act 90 of 2023** brings MSHDA into housing-related approvals, including plans involving school operating taxes and qualifying housing generally at or below **120% AMI**. Eligibility need not depend on contamination. ([EGLE overview](https://www.michigan.gov/egle/about/organization/remediation-and-redevelopment/brownfields/act-381-tax-increment-financing); [MSHDA program](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/developers/tax-increment-financing-tif))

Eligible housing infrastructure can include parking garages, water/sewer and sidewalks; MSHDA’s FAQ permits infrastructure reimbursement beyond the affordable-unit percentage when the required housing connection is demonstrated. ([Housing TIF FAQ, Part II, pp. 2–3](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/-/media/Project/Websites/mshda/developers/tif/Housing-TIF-Frequently-Asked-Questions-Part-II-August-2024.pdf?hash=74A5522B4165EB824FB625FEA756A0D0&rev=f4c5bc16540c4a249ec3b1a203f61815))

EGR’s June 2026 memo described an anticipated Gaslight Housing TIF submission, not an award. The city’s FAQ explains that developers initially fund costs, while approved future increment reimburses them; debt millages and special assessments are excluded. **[inference]** The public-deck bond and private Housing TIF are distinct financing mechanisms. ([June 15 packet, brownfield-authority memo](https://www.eastgrmi.gov/AgendaCenter/ViewFile/Agenda/_06152026-943); [city Housing TIF FAQ, p. 6](https://www.eastgrmi.gov/DocumentCenter/View/4229/Housing-TIF-FAQs-for-City-of-EGR))

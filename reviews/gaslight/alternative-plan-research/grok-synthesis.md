# Grok synthesis: constraints and opportunities for an alternative Gaslight site plan
*Research only, not a plan. Built from codex-constraints-economics.md (C) and claude-community-precedents.md (CL); see those files for citations. Inferences are marked [inference].*

## The starting fact
The 8.6 acres (parcels 41-14-33-276-032 and -029) belong to LLCs whose sole member is Gaslight Investors (C §2). Any alternative plan is therefore a **proposal or vision**. It becomes buildable only if the owner adopts it, the land changes hands, or the city negotiates it into a PUD amendment. Its likely uses are shaping public debate, giving the commission a reference, and giving the owner input. [inference]

## What any plan must satisfy (hard constraints)
1. **Entitlement baseline.** The 2004 Jade Pig PUD still stands: up to 107 condos, 687 parking spaces, a tallest building of 7 stories (79 ft to the roof midpoint, 94 ft to the peak), and a condition to keep a parking structure open for school events (C §1). Anything beyond that needs a PUD amendment.
2. **Vote threshold.** If a qualifying protest petition is filed (owners of 20% of the affected land or of land within 100 ft), an amendment needs 5 of 7 commission votes (MCL 125.3403; C §1). A plan designed to win 5 votes is effectively a political constraint. [inference]
3. **Buildings A and B stay.** They hold 77,500 sq ft of existing retail and their tenants. Their parking demand has to be met.
4. **Parking math is shared.** School displacement (the senior lot going from 111 to 40 spaces), Gaslight customers, private PUD requirements and the Nov 3, 2026 deck bond (up to $9M) all draw on one system. Spaces cannot be double-counted at overlapping peaks (C §3).
5. **Unresolved site risks.** Soils (20–50 ft borings requested, deep foundations possible) and environmental status (opinions only, no Phase I/II) are still open. Utilities have favorable preliminary capacity, but taller buildings may need pressure boosters, and stormwater drains to Reeds Lake (C §2).
6. **Michigan law limits on affordability.** Inclusionary zoning cannot be mandated (MCL 123.411). Attainable units have to come through voluntary agreements, Housing TIF, PILOT or density bonuses (CL §5).
7. **Master Plan consistency.** The plan calls for housing variety at attainable cost, prominent public open space, screened parking and walkability (CL §2).

## Where the real flexibility is
- **Height distribution, more than total units.** Opposition centers on anything over 3–4 stories (CL §1). Stepping down at the edges, with height concentrated at the commercial core, is already a city condition (CL §4).
- **Open space as a public, owned asset.** In the comparable projects, strong greens were publicly owned or funded: Northville's city park, Edina's TIF-funded plaza, Wilmette's village green (CL §3).
- **Parking location and sharing.** Gaslight lots measured 59–72% occupied, so the issue is distribution rather than raw supply (C §3; CL §4). [inference]
- **Financing.** Housing TIF can reimburse garages and infrastructure tied to housing at or below 120% AMI. Kent County's authority has already done this in Lowell (C §4; CL §5).

## The decisions David will have to make
1. **Unit count and mix.** Options range from the entitled 107 condos, to the 147 rentals plus condos last proposed, to fewer and lower. Also decide the owner vs. renter split and whether to aim at seniors and downsizers.
2. **Height.** A cap of 3 or 4 stories, or a stepped profile, and where the tallest mass goes.
3. **Green space.** Size, location (lined up with the Reeds Lake axis or Plaza courtyard), and ownership: dedicated to the city, a public easement, or private.
4. **Parking.** Number of spaces and type (structured, below grade or surface), shared with the schools or not, and whether it relies on, replaces or ignores the public deck on the ballot.
5. **Retail size.** None, small (around 10k sq ft), or the proposed 32k sq ft, and the anchor uses survey respondents wanted (sit-down restaurant, pharmacy, hardware).
6. **Affordability.** Share of units, the income target (100–120% of county AMI vs. lower), and the tool (Housing TIF, PILOT, density bonus).
7. **Phasing and financing.** What gets built first, and whether to accept public incentives such as TIF, which residents distrust.

## Rough economics (screening only; no local bid)
- **Construction cost** [published benchmarks]: wood mid-rise $215–295/sq ft; concrete/steel $285–395/sq ft; structured parking $35–59k per stall; retail shell $165–245/sq ft plus about $120/sq ft for fit-out.
- **Revenue:** Gaslight retail rents $38.50–45/sq ft NNN; village condo sales about $303–530/sq ft (2023–24); EGR median rent $1,845.
- **Codex's illustration:** at $2,500/month rent, 95% collected, 35% operating expenses and a 6% cap rate, a unit is worth about $309k. A roughly 1,000 sq ft gross unit at $250–300/sq ft, before land, parking and fees, costs about the same. So rentals are tight unless parking is cheap or subsidized; high-end condos pencil more easily. [inference]
- **Implication:** cuts in height and density raise the per-unit cost of land and parking. That tension shapes the whole plan. [inference]

## Open questions for the city or the owner
- **Court and appeals:** the outcome of the Oct 9 hearing and any appeal.
- **Owner's intentions:** whether Gaslight Investors would sell, partner, or build under 2004 rights.
- **Agreements:** the executed 2004 PUD exhibits, the setback envelopes, and the deck demolition and parking access agreements.
- **Site tests:** Phase I/II environmental results and soil borings.
- **Parking:** an updated shared-parking and traffic study covering school peaks, and the deck design and operating agreement if the bond passes.
- **Housing TIF:** the status and amount of any submission, and whether EGR would adopt a PILOT ordinance.
- **Public green:** whether the city would fund or accept a dedicated green, and its maintenance cost.
- **Income limits:** the 2026 Kent County AMI rent limits at 80%, 100% and 120%.

# Gaslight Green: An Alternative Plan for the Gaslight Investors Site
*Prepared for David A. Rynties, East Grand Rapids, October 2026. Drafted jointly by Grok Bot, Codex (gpt-6.1-sol) and Claude (claude-opus-5-5) from the research in `reviews/gaslight/`. This is a concept proposal. The 8.6-acre site at 2255 Wealthy St SE / 515 Lakeside Dr SE is privately owned by LLCs of Gaslight Investors, and nothing here can be built unless the owner adopts it or the land changes hands. Inferences are marked [inference]. Screening economics are not bids.*

## Summary
Gaslight Green proposes **116 homes** in buildings of **3 stories at the edges and 4 stories (50 ft including roof peaks) only at the commercial core**. They are arranged around a **1-acre public Village Green** that is built first and protected permanently. The plan also includes a **300-space garage wrapped by buildings** and shared with the school and village, **12,000 sq ft** of new storefronts, and **15 attainable apartments restricted for 30 years** (30% of rentals) *[Revision, Oct 9 2026: "permanently restricted" corrected to "restricted for 30 years"]*. Compared with the voided 147-unit plan, it has 21% fewer homes and is 20 ft lower. Compared with the 2004 entitlement, it is 44 ft lower than the 94 ft roof peak, it has a real public green, and it records parking obligations in an enforceable agreement *[Revision, Oct 9 2026: the claim that the old deck's promises were "never honored" was removed as unsupported. The 2004 PUD §3(j) required keeping and maintaining the structure; the deck was demolished in early 2025 under a city agreement (see alternative-plan-research/codex-constraints-economics.md §1–2).]* It does **not** pencil on market terms. At mid-case screening assumptions, using the City's ~$60k/space garage figure, the gap is about **$27.4M**. A capped Housing TIF, a *negotiated* land/return concession by the owner and a grant for the green could cover about $13.4M of it, leaving **about $14M unfunded**. *[Revision, Oct 9 2026: the gap was recomputed with a $60k garage; it was ~$20.5M with ~$7.3M remaining at $42k/stall]* An independent pro forma review is needed before any vote. It is offered as a public vision and as a basis for negotiation with the owner and the City.

## The seven decisions

| 3 | Green space | **1.25 acres**: a 1.0-acre Village Green **deeded to the City** (fallback: a recorded perpetual public easement) plus a 0.25-acre plaza and rain gardens. Maintenance is funded by a recorded assessment on the PUD, not the general fund. |
| 4 | Parking | **400 spaces**: a 300-stall garage wrapped by buildings, 68 short-term/accessible surface stalls on the internal street, and 32 townhome garage stalls. **145 reserved** for residents (1.25/home); **255 shared** with A/B customers, new retail and school events under a recorded agreement. The plan does not rely on the Nov 3 public-deck bond. |
| 5 | Retail | Keep A/B (77,500 sq ft) and add **12,000 sq ft** of preleased ground-floor space on Wealthy and the green. Targets: sit-down restaurant, café, small-format pharmacy. |

| 7 | Phasing and incentives | **Phase 1** *[Revision, Oct 9 2026: green described as running east toward Reeds Lake]*: the green, the garage and the Wealthy mixed-use building that wraps it (50 rentals plus retail). **Phase 2**: condos and townhomes on Lakeside, after presales. The City hires its own reviewer to confirm the subsidy is truly needed ("but-for" test). No tax abatement. No general-fund cash. |

## Layout in words
- **Wealthy frontage:** Buildings A and B stay. East of them, a 4-story mixed-use building puts storefronts on Wealthy and wraps the garage on three sides, so the garage is not visible from the street.
- **The green:** opens off Wealthy at the site's center and runs **east toward Reeds Lake**, which lies east of the site across Lakeside Dr, with the café/restaurant edge facing it on its west side. *[Revision, Oct 9 2026: orientation corrected; the earlier draft said "south"]*
- **Condos:** a 3-story elevator condo building (two wings, 50 flats) lines the green's east and south edges.
- **Townhomes:** 16 three-story townhomes face Lakeside Dr with rear garages, stepping down to the neighbors.
- **Internal street:** the southern private road becomes a public-access complete street. It can be closed for events, which staff preferred over a pedestrian mall.
- **Garage access:** from the internal street, placed away from school drop-off routes, with final placement set by the required traffic study.

## Unfunded gap and what would have to be true
*[Revision, Oct 9 2026: new section]*

**Math:** cost $92.0M − value $64.6M = gap $27.4M. Then $27.4M − TIF $5.18M − owner concession $6.7M − green grant $1.5M = **about $14.0M unfunded** (about $14.6M if TIF capture is delayed two years).

**Possible levers** (screening estimates, including soft costs and return):
- **Condo pricing at $520/sq ft instead of $480:** about +$2.66M after the 5% sales cost.
- **Smaller garage:** 240 stalls instead of 300 saves about $4.07M net, after adding 60 surface stalls at about $8k each. Keeping **400 total spaces** requires those 60 surface stalls, placed behind buildings A/B and along the internal street (about 0.5 acre), without cutting into the 1.0-acre green. If they can't fit, the total drops to **340 spaces** (shared pool about 195). Either option must pass the school-peak shared-parking study.
- **Value engineering, $225 instead of $235 per sq ft:** about $2.31M.

**All three levers together:** about $9.0M. That still leaves **about $5M short**. For the project to pencil, *all* of the following would have to be true:
1. Every lever above succeeds.
2. And at least one more of these:
   - A larger owner concession (a full waiver of return).
   - About 15–20 more units, which cuts against the density promise.
   - Public parking money tied to shared spaces. This would need voter or commission approval and is **not** covered by the Nov 3 ballot.
   - A garage cost well below the City's figure, which would have to be demonstrated.

**The owner's alternative:** Gaslight Investors may earn more by building under its 2004 rights (107 condos, up to 94 ft, no affordability or green dedication). The land and return concession is therefore a **negotiating ask**. It is not assumed to happen, and it may require something in return: a faster approval path, TIF and certainty.

**TIF per affordable unit:** $5.18M PV ÷ 15 units ≈ **$345k** (≈$333k on the reviewers' rounded $5M). Kent County's first Housing TIF, in Lowell, was about **$140k** per unit. The County BRA should be expected to question this, unless TIF is justified mainly by public infrastructure (the garage and the green) rather than the affordable units.

If the remaining gap cannot be closed, the honest options are a smaller garage with less shared parking, more units, or more public money. The plan should choose among them in public.

Cost benchmarks come from published Detroit/Chicago guides. Revenue benchmarks come from EGR assessor condo sales, Gaslight retail listings and the MSHDA Housing TIF rules (see `alternative-plan-research/codex-constraints-economics.md`).

## Path to five votes [inference]
1. **Keep the four Oct 2025 "yes" votes.** They get housing variety, tax base, new retail and a fast Phase 1 that builds on the Planning Commission's 7–0 Phase 1 work.
2. **Earn a fifth vote by meeting stated concerns on the record:**
   - Density and character (Commissioner Hunter): 21% fewer homes and nothing over 4 stories.
   - Public open space (Commissioner Skaggs's own alternative drawing): a deeded green.
   - Open process (Commissioner Groff-Blaszak's call for a work session).
   - Officials' statement that "this version is not moving forward": this is a different version.
3. **Rebuild trust:**
   - Release the full package 30+ days before any vote: an independent school-session traffic and safety study (the Progressive AE asks), 3D context renderings, the pro forma and the TIF number.
   - Hold a public charrette.
   - Record the parking and school-event obligations so they can be enforced.
4. **Plan for 5 votes regardless.** A protest petition is likely, and lower edges do not change who is eligible to sign one.

## Risks
- The owner declines and keeps its 2004 rights (107 condos, up to 94 ft). It may do better financially that way *[Revision, Oct 9 2026: added]*. The counter is a negotiated package: TIF, more units than 2004 allows, and approval certainty.
- The ~$14M remaining gap *[Revision, Oct 9 2026: was ~$7M]*, the lack of local bids, and soils and environmental results (borings and Phase I/II are still pending) could widen costs.
- The Kent County BRA or MSHDA may reject or shrink the TIF. Residents also distrust TIF.
- Interaction with the Nov 3 deck bond, a court appeal, or a stall after approval (the Okemos precedent).

## Asks
**Of Gaslight Investors:**
- Engage on a revised PUD.
- Share title, easement and environmental information.
- Consider a land/return concession in exchange for a faster approval path.

**Of the City:**
- Commission an independent pro forma and "but-for" review, and a school-peak shared-parking and traffic study.
- Hold an open work session.
- State whether it would accept dedication of a 1-acre green.
- Publish 2026 Kent County rent limits for 80% and 100% AMI.
- Coordinate the deck bond with any shared-garage agreement.

# Alternative plan draft: "Village Green First" for the Gaslight site

*Sources: CE = codex-constraints-economics.md, CP = claude-community-precedents.md, GS = grok-synthesis.md, FB = facebook-sentiment.md, RM = README.md. Inferences are marked [inf].*

## Vision
The site becomes a lower, greener extension of Gaslight Village. A public green is built first, followed by homes for downsizers and young families, a little new retail and one shared garage. The public help is capped and published. The project is shorter and less dense than both the voided 147-unit plan and the 2004 entitlement for 7 stories / 94 ft.

## The 7 decisions
1. **Units and tenure: 118 homes**, about 13.7 per acre (147 were proposed; 107 condos are entitled, per CE §1). That is 68 for sale (50 elevator condos aimed at aging-in-place, plus 18 townhomes) and 50 rentals. Condos make more financial sense than rentals here (GS).
2. **Height:** 3 stories (≤40 ft, the C-1 benchmark in CE §1) along Lakeside and the residential edges. 4 stories (≤52 ft) only on the Wealthy core, with the 4th floor stepped back 10 ft and pitched roofs. This is below the 58 ft Lakewood Hills cap (CP §4) and matches the "3–4 stories" comment cards (CP §1).
3. **Green space: 1.4 acres in total (16%).** A 1.0-acre Village Green is deeded to the City, or covered by a permanent public easement as a fallback. The other 0.4 acre is rain gardens and pocket spaces, privately owned with public access. Maintenance is paid through a recorded assessment on the PUD, not the general fund. This follows staff's Condition 8 and the Master Plan's "prominent open space" (CP §1–2).
4. **Parking: about 420 spaces.**
   - A 300-stall garage wrapped by buildings, 60 surface stalls (accessible and short-term), and 60 townhome garages.
   - Residents get 1.25 reserved stalls per unit. The rest are shared and open to the public, including the 2004 school-event condition (CE §1).
   - The 2025 model showed demand of about 397 for a larger program (CE §3). For this smaller program, demand is about 350 [inf]. A new shared-parking study covering school peaks is required before final approval.
   - No spaces in the proposed public deck (Nov 3 ballot) count toward the private requirement.
5. **Retail:** A and B stay (77,500 sf), plus **15,000 sf** of new ground floor on Wealthy, down from 32,000. It is aimed at a sit-down restaurant, coffee and a pharmacy (CP §2).
6. **Affordability:** **12 rentals, 15-year deed restriction:** 6 at ≤80% AMI and 6 at ≤100% AMI. The tool is a Housing TIF agreement through the Kent County BRA (Brownfield Redevelopment Authority). Michigan law bars mandating affordable units, so this has to be a voluntary agreement (CP §5).
7. **Phasing and incentives:**
   - Phase 1: the green, the garage and a reworked 40-unit Lakeside building (capped at 3 stories), building on the Planning Commission's 7–0 Phase 1 (RM).
   - Phase 2: the Wealthy mixed-use building.
   - Phase 3: the townhomes.
   - A letter of credit guarantees the green and the garage before any Phase 2 permit.

## Site layout
The Village Green sits at the center, aligned on the Reeds Lake axis. It is framed by A/B and the new Wealthy building, with retail facing it. The garage is tucked behind the Wealthy building and wrapped by housing. Its entrance is placed away from school drop-off routes, pending a traffic study. The 3-story condos and townhomes face Lakeside. The southern private road becomes a public complete street that can close for events. Staff advised this instead of a pedestrian mall (CP §1).

## Rough economics [inf, screening only]
- **Cost: about $60–90M** (land excluded).
  - Residential, about 150,000 gross sf at $215–295/sf: $32–44M.
  - Garage, 300 stalls at $35–59k: $10.5–17.7M.
  - New retail: $4–5.5M.
  - Green, site work and utilities: $3–5M.
  - Soft costs: 22–25%.
  - Unit costs from CE §4.
- **Value: about $49–74M.**
  - Sale homes at $303–530/sf, about $29–51M after sales costs.
  - Rentals: about $13–15M, using the $308,750 market-unit benchmark (CE §4) and less for the 12 restricted units.
  - Retail at $38.50–45 NNN: $6.5–8.5M.
- **Gap: mid-case about $15M**, roughly the cost of the garage.
- **How the gap is filled:**
  - Housing TIF capped at **$12M present value**, using only this parcel's own new taxes over 15 years. It reimburses the garage, utilities and the affordable-unit gap, which the MSHDA FAQ allows (CE §4). The Lowell precedent was about $140k per affordable unit (CP §5).
  - The City or grants pay about **$1.5–2M to build the green**. Northville used a $2.5M ARPA grant for its park (CP §3).
  - The owner absorbs the rest through a lower return on land already owned.
  - The City hires its own reviewer to check the pro forma and confirm the subsidy is truly needed ("but-for" test). The TIF amount is published as a single number.

## How it wins 5 votes [inf]
- **Supporters of the 147-unit plan** still get housing variety, tax base, retail and a quick Phase 1.
- **Skeptics** get answers to the concerns they have stated on the record:
  - Hunter: density and character.
  - Skaggs: open space, which was in Skaggs's own alternative drawing (CP §1).
  - Groff-Blaszak: process (asked for an open work session).
- The Mayor and Skaggs said "this version" is not moving forward (CP §1). This is a different version.
- **Process trust:**
  - A full traffic and safety study covering what Progressive AE asked for (CE §3).
  - 3D context models.
  - The garage and school-access obligations are recorded and enforceable, unlike the deck that was demolished (FB).
- 3-story edges should shrink the pool of neighbors within 100 ft who would sign a protest. Even so, plan for 5 votes regardless.

## Risks
- **The owner can refuse** and fall back on 2004 rights (107 condos, 7 stories). The answer is that this plan has more units plus TIF; the 2004 plan has neither.
- **Uncertain numbers.** There is no local bid. Soil borings and Phase I/II environmental work are still pending, and deep foundations would widen the gap (CE §2).
- **Opposition to TIF** (FB), and the risk that the Kent County BRA or MSHDA reject it.
- **City money for the green** and whether a city-owned green is eligible for TIF are unverified.
- **The bond outcome** and any appeal of the court ruling could delay the timeline (RM).
- **A stall like Okemos**, where an approved project went on hold (CP §3).

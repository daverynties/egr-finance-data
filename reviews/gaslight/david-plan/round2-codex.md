**Grok:** Strongest on village layout, garage screening and sharing parking with schools. Its economics are incomplete: $71M excludes land, selling costs and developer return; retail valuation assumes full occupancy without concessions or reserves. Residential area averages just **1,042 gross sf/home**, tight for this mix. The **11 plus 5 affordable units** need clarification. Redirecting public-deck money is an unapproved funding assumption.

**Codex:** Strongest foundation: ownership-heavy housing, permanent green protection, meaningful affordability and explicit subsidy accounting. Its **$77M cost, $70.423M value and $6.577M gap** reconcile. But reducing average ownership prices from $800k to $700k removes **$6.84M**, doubling the gap. At 6%, $8M paid evenly over 15 years has **$5.18M present value**, falling to **$4.61M with a two-year delay**, before administration. Its financing margin is thin; 180 surface spaces also weaken the pedestrian vision.

**Claude:** Strongest on public-space delivery, commissioner-specific concerns and independent financial review. Cost/value ranges broadly reconcile, but **60 townhome garage stalls for 18 homes means 3.33/home**. The **350-space demand** estimate is unsupported. At 6%, **$12M present-value TIF** requires approximately **$1.24M annual capture/$18.53M nominal** over 15 years—before delays or expenses. Lowell’s subsidy per affordable unit establishes neither eligibility nor Gaslight’s tax capacity. Fifteen-year affordability is weak for that subsidy.

**Joint choices I would adopt—provisional until designed and underwritten:**

1. **Units:** Codex’s **112: 56 condos, 16 townhomes, 40 rentals**; 13/acre, 24% below 147.
2. **Height:** Codex’s **4 stories/50 ft to roof peak** at the core; **3/40 ft** at edges; townhomes **35 ft**; fourth-floor setback **12 ft**.
3. **Green:** Codex’s **1.25 contiguous usable acres**, perpetual public easement, owner maintenance and funded reserve.
4. **Parking:** **400 on-site spaces**, initially **220 structured/180 surface**; **140 residential reservations**, leaving 260 shared. Verify A/B, school-event and construction peaks; district vacancies cannot substitute for access.
5. **Retail:** Retain **77,500 sf**; add Codex’s **8,000 sf**, preleased. Avoid speculative restaurant demand.
6. **Affordability:** Codex’s **12 rentals at ≤80% AMI for 30 years**—30% of rentals, 10.7% overall—with utility-inclusive rent limits and annual enforcement.
7. **Phasing/incentives:** Parking, green and rentals first; ownership/retail second, with financing and presales secured. Cap TIF at **$8M nominal/15 years**, contingent on verified capture and [MSHDA approval](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/developers/tax-increment-financing-tif). Require owner equity for any remaining gap.

**What could cost the fifth vote:** unverified school safety, token affordability, paved “green,” taxpayer cash layered onto TIF, or premature claims of feasibility. Shorter buildings do not change protest-petition geography. Owner participation and retaining the previous four supporters remain uncertain; the [voided vote](https://www.eastgrmi.gov/594/Gaslight-Investors) guarantees neither.

# Round 1 — Grok draft: "Gaslight Commons"
**Vision.** Finish the long-vacant site as a walkable, mostly 3–4 story village extension built around a real public green that lines up with Reeds Lake. It should add homes for downsizers and young families without topping the scale people already accept.

## The 7 decisions
1. **Units and tenure:** 120 homes: 70 rental apartments, 30 for-sale condos (senior/downsizer flats) and 20 for-sale townhomes. About 14 units/acre, below the 147 last proposed and above the 2004 PUD's 107 condos.
2. **Height:** 3 stories (≤40 ft) on Lakeside and at all residential edges, with 4 stories (≤52 ft) only at the core next to buildings A and B. Floor 4 steps back 10 ft. No building over 52 ft, which is 18 ft below the 70 ft proposal and well below the 2004 PUD's 94 ft.
3. **Green space:** a 1.0-acre "Village Green" plus a 0.3-acre plaza. Dedicated to the city by public easement; the owner maintains it under the PUD agreement, with city programming rights.
4. **Parking:** 430 spaces total.
   - A 330-space garage tucked behind the core building, wrapped by apartments on its Wealthy side.
   - 40 townhome garages and 60 on-street spaces.
   - A shared-use agreement guarantees 100 public/school-event spaces evenings and weekends. This would let the city shrink or skip the separate public deck.
5. **Retail:** 20,000 sq ft of new ground-floor retail on Wealthy and the green, aimed at the restaurant/café/pharmacy uses survey respondents asked for. That is down from 32,000.
6. **Affordability:** 15% of rentals (11 units) at ≤100% AMI and 5 units at ≤80% AMI, for 30 years, via Kent County BRA Housing TIF (PA 90 of 2023). Written into the PUD agreement, not "up to."
7. **Phasing and incentives:**
   - Phase 1: the garage, the green and the Wealthy mixed-use building. The green is delivered first as a trust-builder.
   - Phase 2: the Lakeside townhomes and condos.
   - Incentives: Housing TIF for the garage, public-space infrastructure and the affordable gap only. No tax abatement.

## Layout in words
- **Wealthy frontage:** buildings A and B stay. East of them, a 4-story mixed-use building (retail plus 50 apartments) wraps the garage.
- **The green:** opens south from Wealthy, on the axis toward Reeds Lake, framed by the café/retail edge.
- **Next to the green:** a 3-story condo building (30 flats) on its east side.
- **Lakeside Dr:** 20 three-story townhomes face Lakeside with rear garages, stepping down toward the neighbors.
- **Phase 2:** a 3-story apartment building (20 units) on the remaining Lakeside corner.
- **Circulation:** the garage is entered from the internal street off Lakeside and Shopping Center Rd, keeping cars out of Wealthy storefront traffic. The internal street is a public-access woonerf that can close for events.

## Rough economics (screening)
- **Cost:**
  - Residential: about 125,000 gross sq ft at $260/sq ft ≈ $33M.
  - Retail: 20k sq ft at $285/sq ft, including fit-out ≈ $5.7M.
  - Garage: 330 stalls at $45k ≈ $15M.
  - Green and site work: ≈ $3M.
  - Soft costs and contingency: 25% ≈ $14M.
  - Total ≈ $71M before land.
- **Value:**
  - 70 rentals at about $2,500 → about $309k each ≈ $21.6M (less the affordable discount, about −$1.5M).
  - 50 for-sale homes at about $650k ≈ $32.5M.
  - Retail: 20k sq ft at $40 NNN ÷ 7% cap ≈ $11.4M.
  - Total ≈ $64M.
- **Gap:** about $7M plus land, roughly 10–15%. The garage and green (≈$18M) are the cause.
- **How the gap is filled:**
  - Housing TIF reimbursement of about $5–7M for the garage and public infrastructure tied to housing.
  - The city puts money it would have spent on a public deck into the 100 shared spaces, for example through a parking easement purchase of about $2–4M, if voters approve.
  - Otherwise, condo pricing or a smaller garage.

## How it wins 5 votes
- **Votes 1–4:** the four "yes" votes from Oct 2025 favored housing and finishing the site, and keep that here.
- **Vote 5:** come from a 2025 "no" vote by meeting their stated concerns: no building over 4 stories, a real public green, density cut by about 18%, committed affordability, a shared parking agreement and a traffic study before approval.
- **Process:** a public charrette and a draft PUD agreement released 30 days before any vote, which rebuilds trust.

## Risks
- The owner may refuse. The plan is a vision unless the owner adopts it or sells.
- A lower unit count may not pencil without TIF, and residents distrust TIF.
- Soils or environmental surprises could raise costs.
- If the deck bond passes, the shared-garage offer could duplicate public parking, so the two need coordination.
- Opponents may still protest; 5 votes are needed either way.

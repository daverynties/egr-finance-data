## Vision

Create a village neighborhood around a permanent public green, with smaller buildings, housing for downsizers and renters, and parking shared with existing businesses. Gaslight Investors would retain ownership and Buildings A/B; this negotiated proposal advances the adopted 2026 Master Plan’s housing, public-space and walkability goals ([research]( ../alternative-plan-research/claude-community-precedents.md)).

## The 7 decisions

*All proposed numbers are planning assumptions, subject to owner agreement and engineering.*

1. **Units and tenure:** **112 homes**: 56 elevator-served condominiums, 16 townhomes and 40 apartments. That is **13 homes/acre**, approximately **24% fewer** than the revised 147-unit proposal ([baseline](../README.md)).

2. **Height:** Maximum **4 stories/50 feet**, including roof peaks, at the commercial core; **3 stories/40 feet** along Lakeside and residential boundaries. Townhomes: **2–2½ stories/35 feet**. Fourth floors step back **12 feet**; contextual renderings must show neighboring homes.

3. **Green space:** **1.25 contiguous acres** of planted, usable green, excluding parking and streets. Gaslight Investors retains title; a recorded perpetual public-access and open-space easement protects it. Owner-funded maintenance, a reserve and city enforcement rights prevent deterioration.

4. **Parking:** Target **400 total on-site spaces**, including existing spaces: **220 in a two-level garage**, **180 surface**. Reserve 112 for homes; share 288 among additional residential demand, A/B customers, new businesses and school events through enforceable schedules. Require updated simultaneous-peak modeling, accessible routes and construction parking before approval. Do not depend on the separate public-deck bond. Existing studies require further verification ([constraints/economics](../alternative-plan-research/codex-constraints-economics.md)).

5. **Retail:** Retain **77,500 square feet** in A/B; add **8,000 square feet**, totaling **85,500**. Favor small storefronts and preleased neighborhood services; restaurant space requires separate parking verification.

6. **Affordability tool:** Voluntarily covenant **12 apartments**, or **30% of rentals**, for households at **≤80% Kent County AMI**, for **30 years**. Gross rent, including utilities, cannot exceed 30% of the applicable income limit. Use Housing TIF, with annual compliance reporting and reimbursement remedies. Any award requires the applicable approvals and [MSHDA review](https://www.michigan.gov/mshda/developers/tax-increment-financing-tif).

7. **Phasing and incentives:** Phase 1 delivers parking, the green and 40 apartments; Phase 2 delivers 72 ownership homes and new retail. Require whole-project financing, adequate condo presales and maintenance security before permits. Proposed TIF ceiling: **$8 million nominal over 15 years**, including administration and financing costs; reimbursement follows verified eligible expenditure.

## Site layout in words

Keep A/B operating. Put the green prominently between existing businesses and new housing, connected to Wealthy and Lakeside by public walking routes. Locate four-story buildings toward the commercial core, townhomes toward residential neighbors, and screen the garage with buildings and trees. Concentrate vehicle access away from school crossings. **[Inference]** This arrangement addresses the dominant height, greenery, parking and trust concerns ([sentiment](../facebook-sentiment.md)).

## Rough economics

**[Inference; screening model, not bids.]** Budget **$77 million**: approximately $40 million residential construction—170,000 gross square feet at $235—$10 million garage, $1 million surface parking, $2.6 million retail, $3.4 million utilities/green, $11 million fees/financing/contingency, $4 million land allocation and $5 million developer return.

Estimated proceeds/value: **$70.4 million**. Assume condos/townhomes average $800,000, less 5% selling costs; apartments average $2,900 market/$1,400 restricted monthly, with 95% collection, 35% operating costs and a 6% capitalization rate; retail contributes $3.6 million. Existing A/B value is excluded.

The **$6.6 million gap** requires a **$2 million owner concession** on land value plus **$4.6 million present value of TIF**. The nominal ceiling must support that discounted value after phasing and expenses. Public help means foregone future tax revenue. Without confirmed eligibility, capture capacity and lender acceptance, this does not pencil ([economic benchmarks](../alternative-plan-research/codex-constraints-economics.md)).

## How it wins 5 votes

**[Inference]** Seek to retain the support behind the previous four votes and earn one opponent through enforceable height limits, a protected green and disclosed subsidy costs. Publish independent school-session traffic analysis, contextual renderings and the pro forma before a fresh hearing; negotiate an owner-supported PUD amendment ([synthesis](../alternative-plan-research/grok-synthesis.md)).

## Risks

Owner refusal; optimistic sales or rents; soils and utility costs; insufficient TIF; parking shortfalls; unresolved litigation. If studies fail, revise units or retail and rerun economics before approval.

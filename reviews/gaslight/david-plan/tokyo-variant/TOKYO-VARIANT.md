# Gaslight Green, "Tokyo-style" variant (yokocho lanes)
*Prepared for David A. Rynties, East Grand Rapids, October 2026. Variant of `../PLAN.md`. Concept proposal only. The 8.6-acre site (2255 Wealthy St SE / 515 Lakeside Dr SE, old Jade Pig site) is privately owned by LLCs of Gaslight Investors. Screening math reuses PLAN.md's unit costs and values; new assumptions are labeled **[assumption]**, judgments **[inference]**. Not bids.*

## Concept
Replace Gaslight Green's few large blocks (one 4-story wrap building, a 2-wing condo building, a townhome row) with **many small buildings on narrow lots** set along **two or three narrow pedestrian lanes** (yokocho-style), each lined with **small ground-floor shops with homes above**. The car footprint shrinks: a **smaller garage at the Wealthy edge** and an **edge surface lot behind Buildings A/B**; the lanes are car-free except for service and emergency access.

Assumption note *[revision]*: GSF is held at 177k even though stated home area falls 8,000 sf; the fine-grain form adds stairs, party walls and circulation, so this is a conservative placeholder, not a design result. The 180-stall garage and the 40/26 for-sale split are design choices, not source requirements. Lane paving and utilities have no separate cost line.

Fixed constraints (unchanged from PLAN.md): height **≤50 ft** (4 stories only at the Wealthy core, 3 elsewhere, townhome-scale edges toward neighbors); the **1.0-acre Village Green** running **east toward Reeds Lake** (the lake is east of the site across Lakeside Dr); **Buildings A and B kept** (77,500 sq ft); **15 attainable rentals restricted for 30 years** (8 at ≤80%, 7 at ≤100% Kent County AMI) through a capped Housing TIF.

## Layout in words
- **Wealthy edge:** A and B stay. East of them, a 4-story building wraps a **180-stall garage** (vs 300) with storefronts on Wealthy; the 50 rentals (incl. the 15 attainable) stay here, as in PLAN.md.
- **Lanes:** two east–west lanes from the garage/A–B side to the green, plus one north–south connector. Lots of roughly shophouse width [assumption: no lot widths are given in the sources; final dimensions need a site survey and fire-code review]. Each building: one shop below, 1–2 homes above, 3 stories.
- **Village Green:** 1.0 acre, deeded to the City, at the lanes' east end, facing Lakeside and the lake.
- **Lakeside edge:** a smaller 3-story elevator condo building (40 flats, aging-in-place) replacing the larger condo wings and the townhome row.
- **Edge parking:** 60 surface stalls behind A/B. *[Revision]* PLAN.md placed its 60 replacement stalls behind A/B **and along the internal street**; this variant removes that street, so fitting all 60 behind A/B is unverified and needs a site survey. No internal-street parking.

## Parking: spaces lost and the shared-parking trade-off
| | Gaslight Green | Tokyo variant |
|---|---|---|
| Garage | 300 | 180 |
| Internal-street surface | 68 | 0 (lanes are car-free) |
| Townhome garages | 32 | 0 |
| Edge surface lot behind A/B | 0 | 60 |
| **Total** | **400** | **240 (−160, −40%)** |
| Resident reserve at 1.25/home | 145 | 145 |
| **Shared pool** (A/B, new retail, school events) | **255** | **95 (−63%)** |
| Shared pool if residents reserved at 1.0/home | 284 | 124 |

Trade-off [inference]: the variant adds 8,000 sf of retail while cutting the shared pool by about 160 spaces. The 2004 PUD §3(j) tied the site to parking for school sporting events and concerts, and the Fleis & VandenBrink model for the 147-unit plan put peak demand near **395–397 spaces**. A 240-space site is well below that; it only works if (a) demand per home and per shop is lower than modeled (walk-in trade, captive share), and (b) the separate Nov 3 public/school deck ($9M ceiling) or district capacity absorbs school-event and overflow peaks. Measured district peaks were 71.7% combined but 86.8% at school locations, so "spare spaces nearby" is not proven at the times that matter. A school-peak shared-parking study is a precondition, not a follow-up. *[Revision after review]* This variant **does** depend on off-site capacity, unlike PLAN.md, which said it did not rely on the Nov 3 bond. That deck is not spare: it exists largely to replace the school's displaced spaces (senior lot 111 → 40, 71 vehicles), and the $9M is a city-borrowing ceiling, not usable overflow; shared spaces cannot be counted twice. The 395–397 model already assumed a 5% retail modal reduction and 20% captive share, so walk-in trade cannot be credited again; the model must be rerun for this program. At the C-1 comparison rate of 1.5/home the reserve is 174 and the shared pool only **66**. §3(j) must also be read together with the later demolition/access agreements.

**Gap: GG ≈ $27.4M → Tokyo ≈ $21.15M** (85.90 − 64.75; hard 62.74 + soft 13.80 + return 5.36 + land 4.0). *[Revision after review: both reviewers caught a $0.12M summing error in the draft (86.02).]* The garage cut alone is worth $7.2M hard, about **$9.4M** with soft costs and return. After the same fills as PLAN.md (TIF $5.18M PV, negotiated owner concession ~$6.7M, green grant ~$1.5M = ~$13.4M), **≈ $7.8M remains unfunded** (vs ≈ $14.0M for GG; +$0.57M if TIF capture is delayed two years). All fills are uncommitted.

**Where the $6.25M improvement comes from:** the smaller garage saves ~$9.4M; everything else nets about **−$3.2M** (10 fewer condos ≈ −$4.0M in value net of townhome/shop-house swap; extra retail adds only ~$0.7M net of its cost).

**Sensitivity (honest downside):**
- Small shops lease at **75%** instead of 90%: value −$1.71M (five of twenty shops dark) → gap **$22.9M**, unfunded ≈ $9.5M.
- Fine-grain penalty (more stairs, elevators, facades, firewalls per home), **+$10/sf** [assumption, mirrors PLAN.md's $10/sf value-engineering lever]: gap **$23.5M**.
- Shop-house homes at **$380/sf** instead of the townhome rate (no garage, no elevator, on a lane; sources' condo range is $303–530/sf): value −$1.38M → gap **$22.5M**.
- Leasing + cost penalty together: gap **$25.2M**, unfunded ≈ $11.8M (still ~$2.2M better than GG).
- The improvement is *parking that is no longer built*. If the shared spaces must be replaced elsewhere with public money, the saving moves to the City's books instead of disappearing [inference].

## Retail leasing risk of many small shops
- 20 small shops is ~7× the tenant count of GG's three anchor targets; each vacancy is visible on a narrow lane, and dead frontage hurts lane walkability more than a big-box gap would [inference].
- Comparable small Gaslight storefronts ask **$38.50–45/sf NNN**; the $40 assumption sits inside that range but no executed leases or concessions were verified.
- *[Revision]* **Car-free lanes are effectively a pedestrian mall.** City staff warned that most pedestrian malls failed and preferred a street that closes for events; this is the variant's biggest retail risk. The $38.50–45 comparables are Wealthy-frontage storefronts; interior lane shops lack that visibility. Shops ≤1,000 sf also cannot hold the pharmacy or hardware uses residents asked for. Option: keep lanes open to slow, timed service traffic and close them for events, matching staff's preference.
- Mitigations: build retail in phases, lane by lane; let ground floors flex to live-work or office use under the PUD; master-lease the lanes to one operator; keep a few anchor uses (café, restaurant) at lane mouths. Pre-leasing thresholds before each phase.

## Votes and politics [inference]
- **Five votes** are still the bar if a qualifying protest petition is filed (MCL 125.3403, two-thirds of seven); the Aug 10, 2026 ruling showed residents can file one.
- *[Revision]* None of the four Oct 2025 "yes" votes is committed; they must be re-earned. Density is unchanged (116 homes, 13.5/acre); smaller buildings change massing, not the count.
- **Possible gains:** Commissioner Hunter's character concerns (smaller massing, though not lower density); Commissioner Skaggs's open-space alternative (green kept, lanes as public space); Commissioner Groff-Blaszak's call for a work session (a new, openly charretted concept); open-space advocates (green kept, lanes as public space); officials' "not moving forward" statements (a different version, though that alone earns no vote).
- **Likely losses:** parking is the sharpest risk. Cutting 160 spaces near a school, after the 2025 deck demolition, collides with §3(j)'s school-event obligation and with the Nov 3 deck vote. Opponents could read it as pushing parking costs onto taxpayers. Merchants in A/B may object to a 95-space shared pool.
- **"Tokyo" branding:** describe it as a village of lanes and small shops; the concept matches EGR's existing Gaslight Village storefront scale better than the label does.
- Same trust steps as PLAN.md: full package 30+ days before any vote, independent pro forma and school-peak parking study, public charrette.

## What would have to be true
1. The owner adopts the variant (it may still prefer its 2004 rights: 107 condos up to 94 ft).
2. A school-peak shared-parking study shows 240 on-site spaces plus district/deck capacity cover simultaneous peaks, with §3(j) obligations satisfied in a recorded agreement.
3. ~20 small shops can be pre-leased near $40 NNN.
4. Fine-grain construction costs stay near PLAN.md's $235/sf.
5. ~$7.8M (or more under the downside cases) is closed by a larger owner concession, more units, or public parking money that voters/commission approve.

**Position: endorse with reservations.** The concept is honestly framed and most figures trace to the sources. But the cost total is wrong, the parking fix counts the same spaces twice, and the draft leaves out staff's warning about pedestrian malls.

1. **The cost total doesn't add up.** The draft's own rows sum to $85.93M, not $86.02M. Recomputed: hard $62.74M; soft at 22% is $13.80M (draft says $13.82M); return at 7% is $5.36M (draft says $5.37M). Corrected total ≈ **$85.90M**, so the gap is **$21.15M** (draft: $21.27M) and the unfunded amount is **≈$7.8M** (draft: $7.9M). Downside cases: 75% leasing gives $22.86M gap and $9.5M unfunded; the +$10/sf penalty gives $23.46M; both give $25.17M and $11.8M unfunded. That last case is still $2.2M better than Gaslight Green's $14.0M, not "close to" it.
2. **"Most of the improvement is parking" understates it.** Cutting the garage saves $9.4M, which is more than the whole $6.25M gain. Everything else nets to about **−$3.2M**: losing 10 condos costs about $4.0M, and the extra retail adds only about $0.7M.
3. **The shop-house price has no source.** $420/sf is the townhome rate, and those townhomes have private garages. These units sit over shops on car-free lanes with no elevator. The sources give a range of $303–530/sf. At $380/sf, value drops about $1.4M.
4. **The edge-lot location is wrong.** PLAN.md put the 60 surface stalls "behind A/B **and along the internal street**." The variant removes the internal street, so the 0.5 acre may not fit.
5. **Parking is counted twice.** The draft leans on the Nov 3 deck, but that deck exists to absorb the school's displaced spaces (the senior lot goes from 111 to 40). It isn't spare. Codex §3 says shared spaces "cannot be counted twice." The variant also drops PLAN's claim that it "does not rely on the Nov 3 bond" without saying so. At C-1's 1.5 spaces/unit, the shared pool would be **66**, not 95. The 240 spaces fall about 155 short of the 395–397 peak modeled for the 147-unit plan. The draft should scale that model to its own program rather than just cite it.
6. **Retail risk misses the biggest precedent.** Car-free lanes are effectively a pedestrian mall. Staff warned that about 90% of pedestrian malls failed and preferred event closures. The $40 rent comparable is a storefront on Wealthy itself; shops inside the lanes won't have that visibility. Shops of 1,000 sf or less also can't house the pharmacy or hardware store survey respondents asked for, and residents already complain about vacancy.
7. **The vote analysis is generic.** It doesn't map gains and losses to named commissioners (Hunter, Skaggs, Groff-Blaszak, Wessely, Schwartz). It cites "this version is not moving forward" but leaves out the Mayor's "the project is not moving forward." It also doesn't test whether the four Oct 2025 "yes" votes would hold.
8. **Minor points:** keeping residential gross area at 177k sf while for-sale floor area falls by 8,000 sf is unexplained, and lane paving and utilities have no cost line.

# Gaslight Village development — Facebook sentiment (EGR community groups)

Researched Oct 9, 2026. Covers public posts and comments in three East Grand Rapids Facebook groups from Apr 2025–Oct 2026 (18 months): EGR Residents' Community Group (~4,000 members), East Grand Rapids Community Group (~6,000), East Grand Rapids Parents (~2,000). Method: paginated group post scans filtered for development keywords, plus comment reads on high-engagement threads. No names recorded except public officials and the developer. Paraphrased throughout.

## Themes

### Top 5 concerns
1. **Scale and character.** The dominant objection: buildings too tall and dense for Gaslight Village ("looks like a college campus," "almost all pavement and no green space"). A supporter noted the tallest building was cut from 7 stories to 5; opponents still call it oversized. One resident compared the proposed 70' height to the 88' performing arts center as "in character"; most disagreed.
2. **Broken PUD promises and process.** Residents repeatedly cite the original ~2004 Gaslight PUD: the developer committed to maintain a parking structure, then let it decay and demolished it — and the city agreed to drop the parking requirement. "The commission does what it wants. Resistance is futile." The Aug 10, 2026 court ruling (Judge Quist, PUD amendment "null and void" over voting-procedure violations) validated process complaints.
3. **Tax incentives for the developer.** A June 2026 post asked whether residents want "a $20M+ tax incentive handed to the Gaslight developer" and why Brownfield authority would go to Kent County instead of the city. Residents note Gaslight Investors says "up to 10%" attainable units (<15); one claims no Michigan Brownfield has ever been approved at 10%. One resident's explainer: Brownfield TIF would reimburse eligible costs from the parcel's own future taxes only — not other residents' taxes.
4. **Traffic and parking near schools.** Drop-off/pickup backups, loss of the parking structure, and whether a traffic study was ever done ("moving forward without a traffic study baffles me"). The parking-deck debate is downstream of this: the developer's demolished ramp is now a taxpayer question.
5. **Green/open space.** Persistent calls for a central green, rain gardens, and community gathering space instead of "more concrete." Post-ruling (Sept 2026), residents floated a "Village Green," farmers market, or city purchase of the lots. Counterpoint: "it's private property — make an offer."

### Top 5 arguments in favor
1. **The site is an eyesore.** "A crumbling former parking ramp and plot of dirt... left abandoned and overgrown for 20 years." Many want something built rather than another 20 years of blight.
2. **Private property rights.** The land was never public green space — it held the Medical Arts building and commercial uses. The owner has a right to develop it for profit; the city can't afford to buy it.
3. **Tax base and revitalization.** Broader tax base, foot traffic for businesses, "revitalize the downtown or lose more businesses." One resident: "sure would be nice to generate tax revenue for the city."
4. **Housing diversity.** Higher density enables more attainable housing and economic diversity; "larger density in targeted areas is better for the environment." (A minority; most affordability claims are met with skepticism.)
5. **The plan already shrank.** Supporters note concessions: 7 stories → 5, scope reduced "in multiple ways based on concerns." The "silent majority" framing appears repeatedly.

### Open questions residents keep asking
- Who pays for cost overruns, and what exactly does the Brownfield application cover? (No dollar figure is in any public post; "$20M+" is unsourced.)
- Will there be a real traffic study before approval, and who enforces the results?
- What happens to the site now that the PUD amendment is void — does the developer build smaller, sue, or sit on it?
- Will the referendum effort continue, or was the court ruling the end of it?
- Why is there virtually no Facebook discussion of the 2026 Master Plan housing changes (Wealthy toward Rosewood, St. Stephen area, Gaslight Village Plaza)? No relevant posts found in 18 months.

## Public-record cross-checks
- **Aug 10, 2026 ruling:** corroborated. Kent County Circuit Court (Judge Quist) found the PUD amendment "null and void" over municipal-law violations in the commission voting process. (Citizen Portal, Aug 2026; city has not announced whether it will appeal; $65,000 in litigation spent per city manager's office.)
- **Referendum petition:** corroborated. A Ballot Question Committee formed Oct 2025 (posts in all three groups); residents reported ~1,500 signatures in three weeks; the protest petition triggered a 5-vote supermajority requirement the commission did not meet. (Citizen Portal reporting of Aug 24, 2026 public comment.)
- **Unit count:** the task's "147 units" vs. the city's Gaslight PUD 5.3.2024 table showing 166 proposed residential units across buildings C–H plus 14 townhomes [Editor's note, Oct 9, 2026: verified. 166 + 14 = 180 is the original May 3, 2024 proposal; later revisions cut it to 149–151 (2025) and then 147 (Aug 27, 2025). See README correction note.], ~10% "attainable" at 100–120% AMI. Discrepancy likely = different phases; flag for verification.
- **"No Michigan Brownfield approved as low as 10%"** — unverified; needs checking against Michigan Strategic Fund records.
- **"20-year tax abatement"** — raised as a question by a resident; not confirmed in any post.
- **"3–3 vote, mayor broke the tie for yes"** — specific claim about a commission vote; checkable against meeting minutes, not verified here.
- **Commissioner Chris Wessely** (public official) posted a video Oct 2025 explaining his vote and "dispelling myths."

## Gaps and caveats
- Facebook's group-post search index only surfaces recent weeks; older posts were found by paginating back to mid-2025. Some threads from the window may be missed.
- Comment reads show top-level comments only; reply threads were not expanded. Counts shown are top-level returns.
- The 2026 Master Plan housing changes drew no detectable discussion in any of the three groups over 18 months — either discussed elsewhere or not yet on residents' radar.
- EGR Marketplace (buy/sell) was not scanned; unlikely to hold civic discussion.

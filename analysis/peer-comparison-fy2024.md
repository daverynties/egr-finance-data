# Peer Comparison — FY2024 Audited Actuals (clean pull, 2026-10-07)

All six cities, same fiscal year (ended June 30, 2024), same definitions, audited
actuals — no budgets mixed in. Figures come from each city's published FY2024
audit/ACFR, governmental funds Statement of Revenues, Expenditures and Changes
in Fund Balances (the source documents underlying Michigan F-65 filings).
"Total" = total governmental fund expenditures; "GF" = General Fund;
"Public safety" = public safety expenditure line (no city breaks out police vs
fire separately); "Debt" = governmental-activities bonded/loan/lease debt,
excluding pension, OPEB, and compensated absences. Component units excluded.
Per-capita uses 2020 Census populations.

Note: EGR's ACFR governmental-funds total ($24.46M) differs slightly from the
FOIA-GL-derived operating figure because of scope (the GL analysis excludes
certain internal-service and fiduciary items). The comparison below uses ACFR
figures for all six cities, so it is apples-to-apples.

## Reading

- EGR spends the most per resident overall ($2,151) and the most per resident
  through its General Fund ($1,331) — roughly double Kentwood's GF per-capita.
- On public safety per resident, EGR ($435) ranks 4th of 6: below Grand Rapids
  ($594), Grandville ($464), and Kentwood ($444); above Walker ($392) and
  Wyoming ($370).
- EGR carries the most debt per resident ($857). Kentwood ($3) and Walker ($4)
  are essentially debt-free; this reflects different capital strategies, not
  necessarily virtue — EGR borrowed for parks (2022) and its municipal complex.
- The pattern: higher taxes, lean-ish spending. EGR's 14.21-mill city levy is
  the highest in the group, but the spending behind it is moderate per resident
  for everything except the fixed costs of running a full-service small city
  (own police, fire, water, parks) with no commercial tax base to share the load.
- Walker levies a city income tax, which is why its property-tax footprint looks
  small; its residents pay through a different channel.

# Peer City Benchmarking — East Grand Rapids vs. Michigan Peers

Research compiled 2026-10-07. All figures are from public records (city budgets,
audits, and the Michigan Department of Treasury). See "How to read this" for
important comparability caveats — figures mix adopted budgets and audited
actuals across fiscal years, and cities define funds differently.

## How to read this (caveats)

- **Budgets vs. actuals are mixed.** EGR and Grandville figures are audited/verified actuals; Grand Rapids, Kentwood, Walker, and Wyoming figures are adopted or proposed budgets. Budgets and actuals differ.
- **Fiscal years differ.** Figures span FY2024 actuals through FY2026 proposed budgets.
- **"Total" vs. General Fund differ by city.** EGR's $26.7M covers all governmental funds plus the water/sewer enterprise; Grand Rapids' $690M is the all-funds budget; Kentwood/Walker/Grandville figures shown are General Fund only. GF per-capita is the most comparable column.
- **Walker levies a city income tax**, which is why its property millage (1.336) looks tiny — its residents pay through income tax instead. Walker also has a proposed 1.75-mill public safety millage on the November 2026 ballot.
- **Wyoming's ~$181M is a proposed FY2026 budget** that explicitly draws on fund balance for ~$60M in capital projects; it is not a normal operating year.
- **Millage shown is the city-controlled portion only** (operating + dedicated city levies), not the total tax bill, which includes schools, county, ISD, GRCC, state education tax, transit, and library.
- Kentwood and Wyoming public-safety spending and Grandville's city millage could not be cleanly verified from indexed sources; marked accordingly rather than estimated.

## How East Grand Rapids compares

On General Fund spending per capita — the most apples-to-apples measure here — East Grand Rapids ($1,203) is the highest of the six, ahead of Walker ($1,096), Grandville ($962), Grand Rapids ($935), and Kentwood ($722). On all-funds spending per capita, EGR ($2,348) sits just below Wyoming's capital-heavy proposed budget (~$2,365) and well below Grand Rapids ($3,469). Public-safety spending per resident is mid-pack: EGR ($413) spends less per capita than Grand Rapids ($566), Walker ($474), and Grandville ($466).

The starkest difference is the city millage rate: EGR's 14.21 mills is the highest in this group (Wyoming ~13.08, Grand Rapids 8.86, Kentwood 6.09, Walker 1.34). That reflects structure, not necessarily generosity — EGR has no city income tax and funds nearly all services through property tax, while Walker leans on income tax and Grand Rapids splits across a broader base. Any "is EGR expensive?" framing should pair the millage with the per-capita spending figures: EGR taxes at a high rate but spends at a moderate per-resident level, consistent with a small, fully built-out residential city with its own police, fire, water, and parks departments and no commercial/industrial tax base to share the load.

**Bottom line for the site feature:** the comparison supports a "higher taxes, lean-ish spending" narrative for EGR relative to nearby peers — but the honest version labels every figure's basis and year, notes Walker's income tax, and treats Wyoming's number as a capital-spike year.

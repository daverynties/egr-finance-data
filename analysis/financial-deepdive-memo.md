# Financial Deep Dive — East Grand Rapids (follow-up analyses, 2026-10-07)

Five new analyses built from the same verified FOIA files. Plain language, exact
figures, no alarmism. A flagged item is not evidence of misconduct — flags mark
spending that differs from budgets, history, or normal patterns and may deserve
further explanation.

## 1. Where the money comes from (revenue.json)

FY2025 operating revenue (excluding interfund transfers): **$30.8M**. The mix:

- **Property taxes: $13.6M** (+5.9% vs FY2024) — the city's largest source, about 44%
  of operating revenue. Growth came from rising taxable values, not higher millages.
- **Charges for services & fees: $6.4M** — dominated by water/sewer bills ($5.3M),
  plus recreation fees (~$1.0M: rec sports, pool, programming).
- **State revenue sharing: ~$1.4M** (constitutional/statutory sales tax) — flat.
- **Interest & investment earnings: $2.5M** — includes pension-trust market gains,
  which swing year to year.
- **Grants: $294K** (down from $583K; ARPA wound down).

Two one-time items flatter the trend: a **$2.05M private donation** to the parks
capital fund in FY2024 (did not repeat) and **$1.735M in bond proceeds** in FY2025
(the February 2025 bond sale — financing, not revenue). Excluding those, underlying
revenue growth is modest and tax-driven.

Sanity check: all-funds revenue exceeded all-funds operating expenditures by
$1.7M in FY2025 ($3.3M in FY2024). The gap reflects bond proceeds, one-time
donations, and fiduciary investment activity — it is not the change in fund
balance. The audited General Fund balance rose $537,519 in FY2025.

## 2. FY2026 budget vs. actual at the 75% mark (budget_variance.json)

Through 3/31/2026 (9 of 12 months), most funds track near the 75% pace. Worth
a citizen's attention:

- **Fleet vehicle purchases: $1,441,260 spent vs. $1,180,000 budgeted (122%).**
  The trucks are identified (sewer truck $567K, loader, excavator, dump trucks).
  Capital budgets are routinely amended and purchases are lumpy — a March
  snapshot is not an overrun finding.
- **Police uniforms: $46,253 spent vs. $28,000 budgeted (165%).** This line was
  previously unverified; it is now confirmed in the YTD file. Small dollars,
  but the largest percentage overrun in the General Fund.
- **Road salt: $67,292 vs. $62,000 (108%)** — winter spending, expected.
- Debt-service funds sit at ~100% (payments are scheduled, not paced). The
  General Fund is at 65% — slightly behind pace, which is normal this early.
- 51 accounts are over budget at the 75% mark overall; nearly all are timing
  (seasonal or lumpy purchases), not evidence of overspending.

## 3. Payroll (payroll.json)

Total city payroll: **$9.03M in FY2025** (−1.0% vs FY2024), about **34% of total
city spending**. By department:

- **Public safety: $3.96M** (44% of all payroll) — but **down $320,115 (−7.5%)**
  from FY2024, the largest departmental change in either direction. The cause
  (positions unfilled, overtime down, or reclassification) is not in these files.
- Next largest: recreation $756K, water/sewer mains & hydrants $623K, finance $551K.
- Recreation payroll rose 9.6% (+$66K); winter maintenance payroll nearly doubled
  (+$49K, a heavy snow year shows up here first).

Payroll is booked in twice-monthly summary entries; individual paycheck detail
is not in the FOIA files.

## 4. Financial health (financial_health.json)

- **Reserves are strong.** The General Fund ended FY2025 at **$7,281,401** —
  about 53% of annual GF spending, more than double the typical 25% municipal
  benchmark. It grew $537,519 during the year.
- **Debt service runs ~$1M/year** ($1,005,485 in FY2025, up from $947,500).
  Recent borrowing: $5.18M (April 2022) and $1.735M (February 2025). Total
  outstanding debt: **needs additional documentation**.
- **Pension:** the city's defined-benefit contribution fell **$1,380,000 →
  $1,100,000**. Whether that reflects improved funding or a policy choice needs
  actuarial data. Net pension liability and funded status: **needs additional
  documentation**.
- **OPEB (retiree health):** liability figure **needs additional documentation**;
  OPEB medical claims tripled ($60K → $188K) for reasons not in the files.
- The proposed **$9M parking bond** (Nov 3, 2026 ballot, not approved) would add
  roughly 1.2% to the homestead tax rate in its first year by arithmetic.

## 5. Water & sewer: does the utility pay for itself? (enterprise.json)

**Yes.** Operating revenues ($5.32M: readiness charges $2.89M, sewage $1.31M,
metered water $909K) exceeded operating expenditures ($4.00M) by **$1.31M, a
24.7% margin** in FY2025 (18.8% in FY2024). Transfers were minor ($287K in, $0
out). The utility is neither subsidized by the General Fund nor subsidizing
other operations. Its largest cost is wholesale water purchased from Grand
Rapids (~$2.0M/year).

---

**Bottom line for residents:** the city takes in slightly more than it spends,
holds reserves well above benchmarks, and runs a self-sustaining water utility.
The two numbers most worth watching into year-end are the fleet vehicle budget
(122% at the 75% mark) and the public-safety payroll drop (−7.5%) — both
deserve a one-sentence explanation at a commission meeting, not suspicion.
The biggest information gaps are the pension funded status and total
outstanding debt, which live in the full ACFR, not in these files.
